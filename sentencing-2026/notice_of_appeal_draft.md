## NOTICE OF APPEAL TO A DISTRICT JUDGE (FED. R. CRIM. P. 58(g)(2))

NOTICE IS HEREBY GIVEN that Defendant Matthew Oliver Reardon, appearing pro se, hereby appeals to a district judge of this Court from the judgment of conviction and sentence entered by the Magistrate Judge on August 7, 2026, following a bench trial finding him guilty on January 16, 2026, of violating 41 C.F.R. § 102-74.390(b), and following the denial of his First Amendment motion to dismiss on July 16, 2026.

### GROUNDS FOR APPEAL

Defendant intends to raise, at minimum, the following issues on appeal:

1. **Spoliation of exculpatory evidence.** The government failed to preserve the entirety of courthouse lobby surveillance footage from the August 25, 2025 incident, footage Defendant contends would show U.S. Marshals directing him toward the door he was subsequently convicted of "obstructing." This implicates *Brady v. Maryland*, 373 U.S. 83 (1963), and *Arizona v. Youngblood*, 488 U.S. 51 (1988).

2. **First Amendment error.** The Court's July 16, 2026 ruling characterizing the courthouse lobby as a nonpublic forum improperly separated Defendant's protected recording/speech activity from the conduct actually charged.

3. **Selective enforcement / entrapment by estoppel.** FOIA production PA-000412 establishes that U.S. Marshals circulated BOLO/District Alert Notices identifying Defendant as a "First Amendment auditor" to Mississippi and Louisiana law enforcement as early as June 2025 — approximately two months before the charged August 25, 2025 incident — and that Marshals had at least two prior contacts with Defendant at the same courthouse (June 23 and June 26, 2025) without arrest, suggesting a pattern of surveillance and eventual selective enforcement rather than an isolated, spontaneous violation.

4. **Sentencing condition — unconstitutional prior restraint.** The sentence imposed includes a special probation condition barring Defendant from the John M. Shaw courthouse and any other federal courthouse in the State of Louisiana, absent "legitimate business" and two business days' advance notice to the U.S. Marshals Service. Defendant objects that this condition operates as an unconstitutional prior restraint on his ongoing work as a journalist and First Amendment auditor, and is broader than reasonably necessary. Defense counsel raised this objection contemporaneously on the record at sentencing; it was overruled.

5. **Sentencing condition — unconstitutional vagueness in the condition's own wording, independent of the "legitimate business" objection already raised and overruled at the hearing.** As orally pronounced, the condition first names the John M. Shaw courthouse specifically, then extends to "any other federal courthouse within the state of Louisiana," then extends again to "any of the grounds adjacent thereto owned or leased by the United States government or any of its departments or agencies" — a clause that, read literally and in isolation, drops the state-specific limitation stated immediately before it. Defendant contends the most defensible construction ties that final clause back to the Louisiana courthouses named earlier in the same sentence, and does not intend by this objection to concede that the condition should be read as a nationwide ban. But a probation condition must give a defendant fair notice of the conduct it prohibits, and this one did not provide that notice clearly even to a listener following closely and in real time at the hearing itself. Defendant requests that, upon entry, the written judgment's exact wording be compared against the court reporter's transcript of the oral pronouncement; any material difference between the two independently supports vacating or remanding the condition for clarification, and the ambiguity in the oral pronouncement itself supports the vagueness objection Defendant's counsel already raised and preserved on the record.

Defendant reserves the right to raise additional issues upon review of the full trial transcript, including matters currently under seal that are the subject of a contemporaneously filed Motion to Unseal.
