MOTION TO SET ORAL ARGUMENT OR EVIDENTIARY HEARING
I. APPELLANT HAS CLEARLY REQUESTED ORAL ARGUMENT
This Court retains discretion to set any case for oral argument pursuant to Miss. R. App.
P. 34(b) when such argument would assist the Court in its consideration of the issues.
Appellant previously noted his request for oral argument in his appellate brief and
reiterated its importance given the factual and constitutional complexity of this matter.
The current record, as outlined in Appellant’s Brief of the Appellant and Motion for
Summary Reversal, demonstrates that such argument is not only warranted—it is
essential.
II. APPELLANT WAS SUPPOSED TO HAVE LEGAL COUNSEL AT ALL
CRITICAL STAGES
The lower court twice dismissed Appellant’s court-appointed counsel during critical
procedural stages, including the filing and perfection of appeal—in direct violation of
Miss. Code Ann. § 99-15-15, M.R.Cr.P. 7.2(c)-(d), and the Sixth and Fourteenth
Amendments of the U.S. Constitution.
The denial of assistance of counsel at the time of filing—notably the phase the court
deemed 'jurisdictionally defective'—is precisely why Appellant was unable to 'perfect' his
appeal in the format now demanded retroactively.
III. THE STATE HAS FAILED TO FILE ANY BRIEF
Pursuant to M.R.A.P. 31(d) and Webber v. State, 197 So. 3d 926 (Miss. Ct. App. 2016),
failure of the Appellee to file a responsive brief is tantamount to a confession of the
errors alleged.
While this Court has denied summary reversal without prejudice, it has not yet provided
an avenue for Appellant to argue these unchallenged errors in open court.
IV. THE JUNE 28, 2024 DISMISSAL ORDER IS BASED ON LEGAL ERROR
The Circuit Court’s dismissal of Appellant’s appeal was predicated on a failure to post
bond or file an affidavit of poverty within 30 days. Yet the record clearly shows that on
December 3, 2021, the Justice Court issued an order waiving all bond requirements due
to Appellant’s indigency. See C.P. 38; Reardon Brief at 3–5.
This waiver was not only on file—it was acknowledged on the record in open court by
Judge Luther himself on April 12, 2022. See Transcript, C.P. 115–116.
The Circuit Court’s later ruling disregarded its own previous findings and, without legal
basis, ruled the court was without jurisdiction—a legal and factual error requiring oral
clarification.
V. COMPOUNDING HARM AND PROCEDURAL OBSTRUCTION DEMAND
OPEN COURT SCRUTINY
Appellant’s appeal has lingered for nearly four years, during which time he has been
subjected to unlawful incarceration, denial of appellate counsel, procedural gatekeeping
by court clerks, and loss of employment opportunities due to the record of conviction
under review.
These ongoing injuries underscore the need for a full and fair public hearing—
particularly given the State’s refusal to respond and the Court’s discretionary power to
suspend procedural rules 'for good cause shown' under M.R.A.P. 2(c).
VI. APPELLANT’S RIGHT TO BE HEARD IS CONSTITUTIONALLY
PROTECTED
The right to be heard is a bedrock principle of Due Process under both the Mississippi
Constitution and the United States Constitution.
Given the unrebutted factual record, the failure of the State to file any brief, and the
serious allegations of judicial bias and systemic neglect, Appellant’s demand for oral
argument is not only appropriate—it is constitutionally required to preserve the
appearance and reality of fairness.
WHEREFORE, PREMISES CONSIDERED
Appellant respectfully requests:
1. That this Court set this case for oral argument pursuant to Rule 34(b);
2. In the alternative, that this Court set the matter for a limited evidentiary hearing to
resolve disputed or mischaracterized facts in the Circuit Court record;
3. That such hearing occur before any further decision is issued on the merits;
4. For any further relief deemed just and proper.
