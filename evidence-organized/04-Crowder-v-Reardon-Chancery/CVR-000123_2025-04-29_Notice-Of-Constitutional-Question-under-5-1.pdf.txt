FEDERAL RULE OF CIVIL PROCEDURE 5.1

COMES NOW Plaintiff Matthew Oliver Reardon, proceeding pro se and in forma
pauperis, and hereby gives notice pursuant to Rule 5.1 of the Federal Rules of Civil
Procedure that he has filed a constitutional challenge to 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2) as
applied to indigent civil rights plaintiffs.
I. NATURE OF THE CONSTITUTIONAL CHALLENGE
Pursuant to Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 5.1(a), Plaintiff identifies the following
specific constitutional challenges to 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2):
1. First Amendment Violation: The mandatory pre-service screening
procedure imposed exclusively upon indigent litigants proceeding in forma
pauperis under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2) violates the First Amendment right to
petition the government for redress of grievances by imposing burdens on
court access that apply solely on the basis of economic status.
2. Fifth Amendment Due Process Violation: The ex parte pre-service
screening procedure deprives indigent litigants of procedural due process by:
o Subjecting their claims to dismissal without prior notice that dismissal
is contemplated;
o Denying them an opportunity to respond or amend before dismissal;
and
1
o Applying a different procedural standard than that afforded to fee-
paying litigants.
3. Fifth Amendment Equal Protection Violation: The screening procedure
violates the equal protection guarantee implicit in the Fifth Amendment's Due
Process Clause by:
o Creating a two-tiered system of justice based solely on economic status;
o Subjecting indigent plaintiffs to heightened pleading scrutiny that fee-
paying plaintiffs do not face; and
o Imposing procedural barriers to court access that cannot withstand
heightened constitutional scrutiny.
These constitutional defects are detailed in Plaintiff's concurrently filed Motion to
Declare 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2) Unconstitutional As Applied and accompanying
Memorandum of Law.
II. PROCEDURAL POSTURE AND URGENCY
This constitutional challenge arises in the context of ongoing civil rights violations
by government officials. On March 31, 2025, this Court stayed all proceedings
pending IFP screening review. On that same day, Plaintiff experienced retaliatory
conduct by federal officials at the FBI Salt Lake City Field Office when attempting
to report misconduct related to the allegations in his Complaint.
2
Due to the Court's stay, Plaintiff has been unable to supplement the record with
evidence of these ongoing constitutional violations, thereby exacerbating the harm
caused by the challenged screening procedure. The constitutional infirmities of the
§ 1915(e)(2) screening process are thus not merely theoretical but are actively
impeding Plaintiff's ability to seek redress for continuing violations of his rights.
III. CERTIFICATION REQUIREMENT UNDER RULE 5.1
Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 5.1(b) requires that "[w]hen the constitutionality of
a federal statute is questioned, the court must promptly certify that fact to the
Attorney General." This certification requirement ensures that the United States has
an opportunity to intervene to defend the constitutionality of its statutes. See Fed. R.
Civ. P. 5.1 Advisory Committee Notes (2006) ("The federal government...should be
notified whenever the constitutionality of an Act of Congress is challenged").
The Supreme Court has recognized the importance of properly certifying
constitutional challenges. See, e.g., United States v. Johnson, 319 U.S. 302, 304
(1943) (emphasizing the importance of proper notification when a federal statute's
constitutionality is challenged).
3
