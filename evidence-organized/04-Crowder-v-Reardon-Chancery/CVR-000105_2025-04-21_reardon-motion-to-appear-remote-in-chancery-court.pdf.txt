DEFENDANT’S MOTION TO APPEAR REMOTELY
IN CONNECTION WITH MOTION TO VACATE RESTRAINING ORDER
COMES NOW the Defendant, Matthew Oliver Reardon, pro se, and respectfully
requests that this Honorable Court allow him to appear via videoconference or
telephonically at any hearing scheduled in connection with his Motion to Vacate
Restraining Order. In support, he states the following:
I. FACTUAL BACKGROUND
1. Defendant currently resides in Layton, Utah, over 1,700 miles from the Lafayette
County Chancery Courthouse in Oxford, Mississippi.
2. The pending motion addresses constitutional violations, procedural due process
issues, and extrinsic fraud arising from the issuance and enforcement of a 2017
restraining order, and does not necessitate in-person witness confrontation at this
stage.
3. Defendant is financially unable to commute such a long distance for preliminary or
non-evidentiary matters, and has no local residence in Mississippi due to the
restraining order's effective banishment effect.
II. LEGAL BASIS FOR REMOTE APPEARANCE
4. Mississippi courts possess discretion to permit remote appearances where good
cause is shown. This discretion has been affirmed and expanded post-COVID,
including in Chancery Courts statewide under administrative orders encouraging use
of remote technology when practicable.
5. Miss. R. Civ. P. 1 mandates a “just, speedy, and inexpensive determination” of all
matters, while Rule 16 gives the Court wide latitude to manage hearings in a manner
that promotes access and judicial efficiency.
6. The Mississippi Supreme Court has recognized that remote appearances may be
allowed in civil and chancery matters, particularly where:
o The movant resides out-of-state;
o Financial hardship would result from travel;
o No cross-examination or live witness conflict arises;
o Remote participation would not prejudice any party.
III. PRESERVATION OF DUE PROCESS
7. Remote appearance will safeguard Defendant’s due process rights while eliminating
the undue burden of unnecessary travel.
8. Defendant is prepared to appear via Zoom, Microsoft Teams, Google Meet, or any
platform authorized by the Court, and will ensure a secure and distraction-free
environment for full participation.
9. There is no prejudice to Petitioners by allowing Defendant to appear remotely; in
fact, remote appearance ensures an efficient and timely adjudication of the
constitutional questions raised.
IV. REQUEST FOR CONDITIONAL APPEARANCE
10. Defendant requests that the Court grant this motion for all preliminary hearings or
non-evidentiary conferences, and revisit the question of physical appearance only if
and when an evidentiary hearing requiring cross-examination is later scheduled.
WHEREFORE, PREMISES CONSIDERED, Defendant respectfully prays that this Court:
1. GRANT leave for Defendant to appear remotely at any hearing or proceeding
connected to the pending Motion to Vacate Restraining Order;
2. AUTHORIZE remote participation via videoconference or telephone on a platform
designated by the Court;
3. ORDER that no adverse inference be drawn from Defendant’s physical absence, and
allow full participation as if appearing in person;
4. GRANT such other and further relief as this Court deems just and proper.
