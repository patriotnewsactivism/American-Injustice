EXECUTIVE SUMMARY (Read This First)
THE CORE ISSUE:
U.S. Marshals sent prejudicial emails about defendant (a journalist) to federal judges two
months before filing criminal charges. FOIA records prove this. The government is now
seeking a protective order to hide which judges received the emails. Yesterday, the magistrate
judge issued a sua sponte interim order before the defense response was even due.
WHY THIS MATTERS:
This is a textbook ex parte communication violation that requires judicial recusal. If the
protective order stands, defendant cannot challenge biased judges, violating due process. The sua
sponte order suggests the magistrate judge herself may have received the communications.
WHAT'S NEEDED:
Legal representation to challenge the protective order, seek judicial recusal, and expose the ex
parte communications scandal.

THE LEGAL VIOLATIONS
1. Ex Parte Communications
Law: Code of Conduct for United States Judges, Canon 3(A)(4)
Violation: U.S. Marshals communicated with judges about defendant before charges filed
Evidence: FOIA records (attached)
Consequence: Any judge who received communications must recuse under 28 U.S.C. § 455(a)
2. Due Process Violation
Law: Fifth Amendment
Violation: Defendant denied knowledge of which judges are biased
Impact: Cannot seek recusal without knowing who received communications
Result: Faces trial before potentially prejudiced tribunal
3. First Amendment Retaliation
Law: First Amendment (Press Clause, Petition Clause)
Violation: Surveillance and prosecution for protected journalism
Pattern: BOLO alert → ex parte communications → arrest → protective order
4. Premature Sua Sponte Order
Law: Due process, Federal Rules of Criminal Procedure
Violation: Magistrate judge acted before defense response due
Impact: Denied defendant opportunity to be heard
Timing: Same day as government motion, week before defense deadline
