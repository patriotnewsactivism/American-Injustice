NOW COMES the Defendant, Matthew Oliver Reardon, and respectfully moves this Court to permit him to
exercise his constitutional right of self-representation under Faretta v. California, 422 U.S. 806 (1975), while
also appointing his current Federal Public Defender as standby/advisory counsel.
1
Grounds for Motion:
1. Defendant knowingly and voluntarily asserts his right to represent himself in these proceedings.
2. Defendant recognizes the challenges of self-representation but seeks direct control of his defense
due to the unique constitutional issues at stake involving press freedoms and retaliation for
journalism.
3. Defendant requests that standby counsel remain available for technical advice, procedural guidance,
and to step in if necessary to maintain orderly proceedings.
4. Allowing hybrid representation will preserve the Defendant’s autonomy while ensuring trial
efficiency and legal accuracy.
WHEREFORE, Defendant respectfully requests this Court permit him to proceed pro se with the assistance of
standby/advisory counsel.

NOTICE OF RETALIATORY TOWING & MOTION FOR SANCTIONS
NOW COMES the Defendant, Matthew Oliver Reardon, and gives notice to this Court of retaliatory conduct
by the United States Marshals Service upon his court-ordered release on August 26, 2025.
Statement of Facts:
1. On August 26, 2025, following a federal judge’s release order, U.S. Marshals contacted R&R Towing
and falsely reported Defendant’s vehicle as parked on private property.
2. Defendant’s vehicle was in fact lawfully parked on a public street adjacent to the courthouse.
3. As a result of this false report, Defendant’s vehicle was towed and impounded, costing $332.48 to
recover, documented by attached receipt (Exhibit A).
4. This retaliatory action was intended to punish Defendant for protected press activity and to drain his
limited financial resources, further impairing his defense.
Grounds for Relief:
1. Such conduct constitutes unlawful retaliation in violation of the First Amendment.
2. The Marshals’ misuse of authority undermines the Court’s release order and prejudices the
Defendant’s ability to prepare for trial.
3. The Court possesses inherent authority to sanction government misconduct and deter further
retaliatory behavior.
Relief Requested:
• Formal recognition of retaliatory conduct on the record.
• Sanctions or corrective measures as deemed appropriate by the Court.
• Reimbursement of $332.48 to Defendant for wrongful impoundment.
• Such further relief as this Court deems just and proper.
Respectfully submitted,
Matthew Oliver Reardon, Pro Se
