51. This Court must rule on Mr. Reardon's Motion to Recuse within thirty (30) days of the 
filing of the Motion pursuant to Uniform Chancery Court Rule 1.11. Mr. Reardon did 
not attempt to obtain a setting date for this Motion from the Court Administrator when he 
hand delivered his Motion. However, the Court is of the opinion that a hearing on this 
Motion is not necessary as Mr. Reardon thoroughly raised his issues with the Court in his 
written Motion. Further, all previously raised issues that he has reasserted in this Motion 
were adjudicated by agreement of the Parties and now MOOT. Therefore the Court files 
this Order. 
52. First, Mr. Reardon argues that this is his second (2nd) attempt in asking this Court to 
recuse from his case. The Court is not aware of any previous request for recusal by Mr. 
Reardon. A Motion for Recusal has never been filed or served upon the Court by Mr. 
Reardon. However, if one was, it was moot as all proceedings that were previously set 
before this Court were resolved by agreement of the Parties and therefore waived. 
Therefore the Court considers this Motion to be Mr. Reardon's first request for its 
recusal. 
53. Mr. Reardon argues that: 
Under the Mississippi Rules of Criminal Procedure Defendant now faces Double Jeopardy based on the same set of facts asserted and forcing the Defendant to waive his Fourth Amendment Right and in doing so forgo his [F]ifth [A]mendment [R]ight against self-incrimination by ordering Defendant to prove his innocence and give evidence to the State, or forgo visitation with his minor child. Underserved expoitation of the minor child, by way of making a presumable innocent man and father whom has ongoing matters involving alleged civil rights violations, choose between 
protecting his civil rights seemingly further under siege or surrender his rights in an apparent barter for due time with his minor child specified out in the current child visitation agreement with which is further supported by his continued compliance with child support obligations continuously being paid to petitioner pursuant to the current child support agreement and subsequent withholding order. The purposes served by Rule 2.2 are in accord with Mississippi law and the mandates of the Fourth Amendment to the United States Constitution. 
54. The Court is sympathetic to Mr. Reardon and his argument but it is completely without 
merit. First of all, the Court is unaware of the facts and circumstances that Mr. Reardon 
is concerned about causing him Double Jeopardy, or being a violation of his Fourth and 
Fifth Amendment Rights. He was ordered to take a nail bed drug test on September 14, 
2021 due to a charge of paraphernalia in Calhoun County, Mississippi. According to Mr. 
Reardon and Ms. Pittman, this charge has been retired to the file pending Mr. Reardon's 
good behavior in Calhoun County. The reason for this Court to Order the nail bed drug 
test on Mr. Reardon is related to that charge in the sense that he received it, but not to 
prove that charge or incriminate him of that charge. Mr. Reardon is a father of a minor 
child who had visitation rights. That means he has periods of time that he is allowed to 
have unsupervised and unfettered visitation with his minor child. If there is any reason 
that this Court believes he might be using or taking illegal drugs, then it is within its 
purview to order drug testing. Further, innocence and guilt have no place in Chancery 
Court. Instead, this Court considers what is in the best interest of the child, and the best 
interest of the child is to have visitation with a parent that is not using illegal drugs. Once 
Mr. Reardon can show that he is not using illegal drugs, he can resume his visitation. It 
is up to him, take the drug test, test negative, and get visitation; test positive, and no 
visitation until a negative drug test can be obtained by him. The Court finds this 
argument without merit. 
55. Mr. Reardon next argues that he has raised the same grievances over and over about Ms. 
Kester in regard to her allegedly withholding the minor child. He states that: "[E]ach 
motion brought before this court has been prejudicially ignored with preferential 
treatment shown to the petitioner ..." 
56. Mr. Reardon has previously asserted counterclaims against Ms.Kester, but has mutually 
agreed to resolve them by Agreed Order either on the date of the hearing or just prior to 
it. Mr. Reardon has also failed on numerous occasions outline above to properly serve 
and set his grievances. Five actions have been initiated properly by Ms. Kester. One 
action was attempted to be initiated by Mr. Reardon but he failed in doing so. The Court 
finds this argument without merit. 
57. Next, Mr. Reardon argues that he has been denied Due Process under the Fourteenth 
Amendment. As outlined above, Mr. Reardon has received Due Process and this 
argument is without merit. 
58. Mr. Reardon's Motion for Recusal is therefore DENIED. 
59. All other matters pending in his Motion for Dismissal filed on September 28, 2021 shall 
be set for hearing by Mr. Reardon at a later date. He will need to contact this Court's
