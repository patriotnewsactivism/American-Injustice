I. ARGUMENT
A. Plaintiff’s Response is Untimely.
As a threshold matter, Plaintiff’s response in opposition is untimely. County Defendants
filed their Motion for Judgment on the Pleadings on January 11, 2023 (Doc. 46), and Plaintiff’s
response was due to be filed by January 25, 2023 pursuant to the local rules. L.U.C.R. 7(b)(4)
(“Counsel for respondent must, within fourteen days after service of movant’s motion and
memorandum brief, file a response and memorandum brief in support of the response.”). Plaintiff
filed his response in opposition on March 17, 2023, nearly two months after the deadline. Doc.

B. Plaintiff’s Constitutional Claims Fail on their Face.
Plaintiff’s oppositional response misses the mark in pleading a plausible basis for his
constitutional claims. With respect to his constitutional claims against the County, Plaintiff’s
response, at best, merely recites the elements of his constitutional claims against the County;
however, Plaintiff’s response does not allege that any alleged deprivation of his First, Fourth, Fifth,
and Fourteenth Amendment rights resulted from an official policy, custom, or practice of the
County. See Monell v. Dept. of Soc. Servs., 436 U.S. 658, 690-91 (1978). Both the Amended
Complaint and Plaintiff’s oppositional response are entirely silent as to any official policy,
practice, or custom of the County or the Sheriff’s Department. This glaring deficiency is a
dispositive ground for dismissal of Plaintiff’s constitutional claims against the County. Id.

Moreover, as with the Amended Complaint, Plaintiff’s constitutional claims against Sheriff
East in his individual capacity fail because Plaintiff has not plausibly pled that Sheriff East violated
“clearly established law” under the First, Fourth, Fifth, or Fifteenth Amendment as necessary to
overcome qualified immunity. See Carr v. Hoover, No. 4:16-cv-88-MPM-DAS, 2018 WL
3636563 (N.D. Miss. July 31, 2018), affirmed by 837 Fed. Appx. 279 (5th Cir. 2020). Specifically,
Plaintiff has not offered any case that is both “authoritative and specific” in support of his argument
that Sheriff East violated “clearly established law.” Id. at *8 (the determination of whether an
official’s conduct violated clearly established law “requires the court to inquire whether the
identified case is both ‘authoritative’ and ‘specific.’”). Therefore, Sheriff East is entitled to
qualified immunity, and Plaintiff’s federal claims against Sheriff East should be dismissed
accordingly.
C. Plaintiff’s State Law Claims Fail on Their Face.
As explained in County Defendants’ opening brief, Plaintiff’s state law claims for false
imprisonment and negligent infliction of emotional distress fail as a matter of law because Plaintiff
failed to provide pre-suit notice of those claims as required by Mississippi Code § 11-46-11. Miss.
Code Ann. § 11-46-11 (requiring plaintiffs asserting claims against a governmental entity to “file
a notice of claim with the chief executive officer of the governmental entity” at least 90 days before
filing suit.”); Univ. of Miss. Med. Ctr. v. Easterling, 928 So. 2d 815, 818 (Miss. 2006). Plaintiff
contends that he provided the requisite pre-suit notice by serving an “Affidavit of Truth.” Doc.
70, at 2-3. Notably, the “Affidavit of Truth” makes no reference to the Mississippi Tort Claims
Act, nor does it refer to any of the specific state law claims Plaintiffs asserted in the Amended
Complaint (or the allegations giving rise thereto). Additionally, the Amended Complaint makes
much of the notion that Sheriff East did not respond to the “Affidavit of Truth,” further evidencing

that Plaintiff did not intend for the “Affidavit of Truth” to serve as the notice required under
Mississippi Code § 11-46-11. Doc. 30, at 28. Most importantly, Plaintiff does not cite any
authority supporting his argument that the “Affidavit of Truth” satisfies the pre-suit notice
requirements of the MTCA.
Even if the Court liberally construes the “Affidavit of Truth” as sufficient pre-suit notice
under the MTCA, Plaintiff’s state law claims still fail. With respect to Plaintiff’s claims for false
imprisonment and negligent infliction of emotional distress, those claims are substantively barred
by the MTCA. See Bell v. Miss. Dept. of Human Servs., 126 So. 3d 999 (Miss. Ct. App. 2013)
(applying MTCA to false imprisonment claim); Sloan v. Shannon, 2009 WL 1162639, at *11 (N.D.
Miss. Apr. 29, 2009) (applying MTCA to claim for negligent infliction of emotional distress). As
for Plaintiff’s remaining state law claims for defamation, tortious interference with business
relations, and intentional infliction of emotional distress, Plaintiff’s oppositional response contains
no plausible allegation necessary to sufficiently plead any of those claims. The Court should
likewise dismiss each of Plaintiff’s state law claims.
II. CONCLUSION
For each of the foregoing reasons, and for the reasons asserted in County Defendants’
motion for judgment on the pleadings (Doc. 46), County Defendants respectfully request that the
Court dismiss Plaintiff’s Amended Complaint with prejudice.
