RESPONSE TO ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE
I. Intimidation of a Witness Charge
1. On November 22, 2022, Plaintiff was arrested in Lowndes County,
Mississippi, for intimidation of a witness.
2. The charge was initially filed under Miss. Code Ann. § 97-9-113, which
carried a maximum penalty of five years.
3. When bond was about to be granted, the District Attorney dismissed this
charge.
4. Immediately thereafter, law enforcement re-filed the same alleged conduct
under a different statute carrying a higher penalty of fifteen years.
5. In March 2023, both charges were presented to the same grand jury. The
grand jury returned a no-bill on the refiled charge and the original charge
remained dismissed.
6. Thus, both intimidation charges terminated in Plaintiff’s favor—one by
dismissal and the other by no-bill. Under Thompson v. Clark, 596 U.S. 36
(2022), dismissal and no-bill are sufficient to establish favorable termination.
II. Retaliation Against a Public Servant Charge
7. On or about February 1, 2023, Investigator Jeff Edmonson presented Plaintiff
with a new charge of retaliation against a public servant.
8. Plaintiff never received an initial appearance or preliminary hearing on this
charge.
9. A purported grand jury indictment was later issued on March 10, 2023.
Plaintiff believes this instrument was irregular and improperly generated.
10. Ultimately, this charge was retired to the file and has never been prosecuted
further.
11. A charge that is retired to the file leaves no conviction and no pending
prosecution. Plaintiff submits that this constitutes termination in her favor,
or at a minimum should be treated as such for purposes of malicious
prosecution.
III. Lack of Probable Cause
12. Although a justice court judge made an initial probable cause finding on the
intimidation charge at Plaintiff’s first appearance, subsequent events negated
probable cause:
a) The District Attorney himself dismissed the charge when bond was
imminent;
b) The grand jury later returned a no-bill on the refiled version of the same
allegation; and
c) The record shows manipulation of charging instruments by the Sheriff’s
Department, undermining the legitimacy of the proceedings.
13. These facts collectively demonstrate that the charges lacked probable cause
and were fabricated to retaliate against Plaintiff.
IV. Conclusion
14. Plaintiff has now provided the clarifications requested by the Court:
a) The intimidation charges were dismissed/no-billed — favorable
termination.
b) The retaliation charge was retired to the file — no conviction, no
pending prosecution.
c) These facts satisfy the elements of malicious prosecution under §
1983 as recognized in Thompson v. Clark.
WHEREFORE, Plaintiff respectfully requests that this Court accept the foregoing
clarifications, find that Plaintiff has stated a claim upon which relief can be granted,
and permit this case to proceed on the merits.
