## 1. Prepared federal allocution — August 7, 2026

**Uploaded source:** `cd236cf3e_ALLOCUTION_large_print.pdf` — 29 pages.  
**Repository transcription:** `sentencing-2026/prepared_allocution_2026-08-07.md`.

The document is the prepared large-print allocution for *United States v. Matthew Oliver Reardon*, 6:25-CR-00227. It identifies the hearing date as August 7, 2026 and contains delivery instructions targeting approximately nineteen to twenty minutes.

The August 7 public livestream confirms that Reardon actually delivered an allocution beginning at approximately 27:50 and followed the prepared text closely. The prepared statement is therefore useful for exact intended wording, while the livestream and official transcript control what was actually spoken.

Important distinction: allegations in the allocution concerning an unlawful campaign, judicial prejudice, deliberate concealment or destruction of evidence, kidnapping, assault, equipment destruction, and other criminal wrongdoing are sentencing advocacy unless independently established by public records or adjudicated findings.

## 2. Orleans Parish / NOPD warrant and booking packet

**Uploaded source:** `Reardon, Matthew-K1120525.pdf` — 7 pages.

The packet contains:

- Orleans Parish Sheriff arrest register for the January 28, 2026 booking;
- NOPD initial incident material;
- sworn affidavit for arrest warrant K-11205-25;
- arrest warrant signed November 12, 2025;
- NOPD warrant-entry report.

### Key verified facts

- The warrant affidavit places the November 12 City Hall incident at approximately **9:23 a.m.** at 1300 Perdido Street.
- Officers Grady and Paz arrived and found Reardon lying face down on the sidewalk and bleeding from the back of his head.
- Security guard Jerome Ard told police Reardon tried to bypass the scanner after being told a lighter/vape could not enter; Ard alleged Reardon slapped his hands and bumped/shoved him; Ard said he pushed Reardon away and Reardon fell and struck his head.
- Security personnel Wilfred Vidal and Willie Savage gave materially similar accounts according to the affidavit.
- The affidavit states that RTCC footage showed portions of the security-checkpoint lead-up but that the **physical altercation was outside the camera's field of view**.
- Paz identified Reardon as the aggressor after witness interviews and review of available video; Grady swore the warrant affidavit.
- Judge Juana Marine Lombard approved the misdemeanor simple-battery warrant at 3:34 p.m. November 12, 2025.
- The affidavit says EMS transported Reardon to Tulane for further medical attention.
- The January 28 arrest register lists the Orleans booking bond amount as **$0.00**.

### Preserved record conflict

The later arrest register lists the offense time as **9:23 p.m.** The sworn warrant affidavit identifies approximately **9:23 a.m.**, and Reardon's November 17 records request sought records from **9:00–10:30 a.m.** The manuscript treats this as an unreconciled a.m./p.m. inconsistency rather than silently correcting one record.

## 4. Mississippi LK22-358 packet

**Uploaded source:** `M. Reardon.pdf` — 13 pages.

The packet includes the 2022 aggravated-stalking indictment, September 30 sentencing order, Notices of Criminal Disposition, November 3 revocation order, November 14 order denying withdrawal of guilty plea, 2023 bench warrant, and 2023 petition to revoke.

### Key verified facts

- The LK22-358 indictment was filed **September 6, 2022**.
- It alleges aggravated stalking **“on or about June 24, 2022”** directed at Robyn Tannehill and invokes the prior aggravated-stalking conviction within seven years.
- It identifies the earlier conviction as LK17-295, conviction date July 6, 2017, and states the earlier offense occurred May 8, 2017.
- Shane Fortner and Joey Mauney are listed as witnesses on the indictment.
- The signed September 30, 2022 sentencing order imposes five years suspended, zero immediately to serve, five years unsupervised probation, five-year banishment, forty-five days to relocate, no contact, and a 365-day right of review.
- Monetary assessment: **$592.50 court costs + $250.00 fine = $842.50 total**. The total had previously been described incorrectly as court costs alone.
- The November 3, 2022 revocation order imposes one year to serve and continues banishment from Lafayette County.
- The November 14 order denying withdrawal of guilty plea relies on the plea colloquy, counsel-satisfaction statements, denial of coercion, admission of guilt, and competency evaluation.

### Preserved record conflicts

- MBI's earlier affidavit centers the new investigation on the **June 20, 2022** City Hall visit, while the indictment alleges the offense **“on or about June 24, 2022.”**
- A later 2023 petition to revoke states that Reardon was sentenced on **September 20, 2022**, while the signed sentencing order, Notice of Criminal Disposition, and November 2022 transcript establish **September 30, 2022**. The manuscript uses September 30 and flags September 20 as a later clerical inconsistency.
