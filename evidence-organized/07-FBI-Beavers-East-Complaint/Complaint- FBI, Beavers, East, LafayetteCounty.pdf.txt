INTRODUCTION
1. This complaint seeks to compel the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) to fulfill its
statutory duty to investigate allegations of law enforcement misconduct and obstruction
of justice in accordance with federal law.
2. This action also seeks legal accountability for Sheriff’s Deputy Kandace Beavers,
Lafayette County, Mississippi, and Sheriff Joey East for their roles in wrongful
conviction, perjury, conspiracy, malicious prosecution, and civil rights violations.
3. The FBI has failed to act on credible allegations of police misconduct involving the Holly
Springs Police Department, as well as credible allegations of police misconduct and
public corruption involving Lafayette County, Mississippi, despite the plaintiff’s attempt
to report said misconduct on October 28, 2022.
4. Beavers, acting under the color of law, falsely testified that petitioner had no lawful
reason to visit the FBI office, contradicting recorded phone calls proving otherwise.
5. Lafayette County and Sheriff Joey East facilitated and failed to prevent Beavers’ perjury,
contributing to the wrongful revocation and imprisonment of the petitioner.
6. As a direct result of these actions, petitioner was wrongfully sentenced to one year in
prison, constituting violations of due process and civil rights.
JURISDICTION AND VENUE
7. This Court has jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1361 (Mandamus), 5 U.S.C. § 706
(Administrative Procedure Act), 28 U.S.C. § 1331 (Federal Question Jurisdiction), and 42
U.S.C. §§ 1983, 1985 (Civil Rights Violations & Conspiracy).
8. Venue is proper under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b) because the events giving rise to this claim
occurred within this district, and the respondents have offices within the jurisdiction of
this Court.

FACTUAL BACKGROUND
14. On October 28, 2022, Matthew Reardon contacted the FBI office in Oxford, Mississippi,
seeking to report alleged misconduct by the Holly Springs Police Department, as well as
credible allegations of police misconduct and public corruption involving Lafayette
County, Mississippi.
15. Reardon had been informed that FBI agents would be available but, upon arrival, found
no agents present and was subsequently denied an opportunity to report the misconduct
despite pre-arranging an approximate time with an employee of the local FBI Branch.
16. Prior to visiting the FBI, Reardon followed the legal protocol of his “banishment” from
Lafayette County, Mississippi by informing Deputy Kandace Beavers of his intent to
enter the city of Oxford to meet with the FBI.
17. The FBI employee confirmed during a recorded phone call that walk-ins were allowed
and suggested that Reardon visit between 8 AM and 4 PM, preferably before noon.
18. A separate recorded call from Deputy Beavers confirms that Reardon notified local law
enforcement of his intent to visit the FBI and was not given any indication that this would
violate his probation.
19. In a third phone call (originating from Beavers) Deputy Beavers reconfirmed that she
understood Reardon’s planned visit and merely asked him to notify the Sheriff’s
Department if he decided to go anywhere else, further proving that law enforcement did
not object to his FBI visit.
20. After leaving the FBI office, Reardon immediately notified Deputy Beavers that he was
heading to the U.S. Attorney’s Office to escalate his grievance since the FBI was
unavailable.
21. Despite following FBI instructions and complying with notification requirements,
Reardon’s attempt to report misconduct was later used against him in a probation
revocation motion, which cited his visit to the FBI office as a violation of his probation.
22. Deputy Kandace Beavers provided false testimony during court proceedings, claiming
that the FBI had confirmed Reardon had no appointment and no legitimate purpose for
visiting. However, the recorded phone calls with both the FBI and Beavers explicitly
demonstrate that Reardon acted according to the instructions provided by the FBI.
23. Lafayette County and Sheriff Joey East failed to prevent or investigate Beavers’ perjury,
making them complicit in Reardon’s wrongful imprisonment.
LEGAL CLAIMS
Count I: Violation of the Administrative Procedure Act (5 U.S.C. § 706) – Against the FBI
24. The FBI had a duty to investigate allegations of public corruption and civil rights
violations.
25. The FBI failed in its duty to investigate allegations of police corruption, constituting an
unlawful withholding of agency action.
26. The FBI failed to act despite clear, documented evidence of misconduct.
27. Under the Administrative Procedure Act (APA), courts can compel an agency to act
when it has unreasonably delayed or unlawfully withheld action.
Count II: Writ of Mandamus (28 U.S.C. § 1361) – Against the FBI
25. The FBI has a clear, non-discretionary duty to investigate police misconduct and public
corruption under 18 U.S.C. § 242.
26. By failing to take action, the FBI has abdicated its legal responsibility.
27. A writ of mandamus is necessary to compel the FBI to act.
Count III: Civil Rights Violation Under 42 U.S.C. § 1983 – Against Beavers & Lafayette
County
26. Deputy Beavers acted under color of law when she knowingly provided false testimony
in court.
27. Lafayette County is responsible for the actions of its officers and officials under Monell
liability (Monell v. Department of Social Services, 1978).
28. Beavers’ false testimony directly led to Reardon’s wrongful conviction, violating due
process rights under the Fifth and Fourteenth Amendments
Count IV: Conspiracy to Violate Civil Rights (42 U.S.C. § 1985) – Against Beavers, Sheriff
East & Lafayette County
29. Lafayette County officials and Beavers conspired to revoke Reardon’s probation based
on false claims.
30. Evidence shows coordinated actions between the Sheriff’s Department, Lafayette County
prosecutors, and Beavers to deprive Reardon of his constitutional rights.
31. 42 U.S.C. § 1985(3) prohibits conspiracies aimed at depriving individuals of equal
protection under the law.
Count V: Malicious Prosecution – Against Beavers, Sheriff East & Lafayette County
32. Reardon was subjected to prosecution based on knowingly false allegations.
33. Beavers’ false testimony was a key factor in the wrongful conviction.
34. Lafayette County allowed and facilitated this malicious prosecution.
Count VI: False Imprisonment – Against Lafayette County & Sheriff East
35. Reardon was unlawfully detained and sentenced to one year in prison based on fabricated
testimony.
36. Lafayette County failed in its duty to ensure due process.
37. The imprisonment violated constitutional rights under the Fourth, Fifth, and Fourteenth
Amendments.
Count VII: Negligent Supervision & Failure to Train (Monell Claim) – Against Lafayette
County
38. Lafayette County failed to properly train or supervise Deputy Beavers, leading to her
false testimony.
39. The county’s lack of oversight directly contributed to Reardon’s wrongful imprisonment.
40. Under Monell v. Dept. of Social Services, a municipality can be held liable for failing to
train its officers when it results in constitutional violations.
Count VIII: Obstruction of Justice (18 U.S.C. § 1503, § 1513) – Against Beavers
41. Beavers provided false testimony in a legal proceeding to secure a wrongful conviction.
42. This constitutes obstruction of justice and retaliation against a witness.

4. Award damages, both compensatory and punitive, for the malicious prosecution,
