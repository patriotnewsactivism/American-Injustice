STATE’S REQUEST FOR RECONSIDERATION OF ORDER DISMISSING
CASE WITH PREJUDICE
Comes now the State of Alabama by and through its Attorney General Luther Strange
and respectfully requests this Honorable Court to reconsider its order on January 22, 2016,
dismissing the above style case with prejudice. As grounds for this request that State avers the
following:
1. The Defendant was charged with a capital offense in the above style cause with
regard to causing the death of Donald Cameron while in the commission of a burglary.
2. On Wednesday January 20, 2016, the State filed a “Motion to Dismiss”1 the
above styled matter. The necessity for dismissal became apparent to the State as it was preparing
for the February 29, 2016 trial date. During the course of its preparation the State became aware
that the May 13, 2013, indictment failed to apprise the defendant of each and every element of
the charge of Capital Murder during the course of a Burglary in the First or Second Degree as set
forth in 13A-5-40(a)(4). Specifically, the May 13, 2013 charging instrument reads:
The Grand Jury of Calhoun County charges that before the finding
of this Indictment, MICHAEL WILLIAM KISER, whose name

Dictionary defines the phrase “with prejudice” as “[w]ith loss of all rights; in a way that finally
disposes of a party's claim and bars any future action on that claim.” Black's Law Dictionary
1837 (10th ed.2014) (emphasis added). Black's defines “dismissal with prejudice” as“[a]
dismissal, usu. after an adjudication on the merits, barring the plaintiff from prosecuting any
later lawsuit on the same claim. If, after a dismissal with prejudice, the plaintiff files a later suit
on the same claim, the defendant in the later suit can assert the defense of res judicata (claim
preclusion).” Black's Law Dictionary 569 (10th ed.2014).” Emphasis added.)
5. While the State recognizes that the Court is the final authority with respect to the
ultimate disposition of a case, it respectfully asks for reconsideration and modification of the
Court’s Order to a nolle prosecui (or dismissal “without prejudice”) due to the nature of this case
and the impact dismissal “with prejudice” will have on the State’s ability to seek justice in this
matter.
