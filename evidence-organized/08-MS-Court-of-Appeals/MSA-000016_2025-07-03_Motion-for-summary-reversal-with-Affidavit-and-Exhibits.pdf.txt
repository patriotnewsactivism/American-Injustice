I. FAILURE TO FILE BRIEF – CONCESSION OF ERROR
1. On June 10, 2025, the Clerk of the Mississippi Supreme Court formally notified
Appellee counsel that the brief of Appellee had not been filed within the time
required by the Mississippi Rules of Appellate Procedure.
2. Pursuant to M.R.A.P. 31(d):
“If an appellee fails to file the appellee’s brief as required, such brief, if later
filed, may be stricken from the record… An appellee who fails to file a brief
will not be heard at oral argument except by permission of the court.”
3. Appellee’s failure to respond or defend the judgment below constitutes a waiver of
argument and a de facto concession of error. Mississippi appellate courts have long
held that failure to file a responsive brief allows the Court to reverse the lower
court’s ruling as unopposed and proceed to grant relief based on the Appellant’s
arguments and supporting record.
4. The appellate record—including unrebutted procedural and constitutional claims,
judicial admissions in the transcript, and a well-supported brief—establishes that
the Circuit Court’s dismissal of Appellant’s appeal was: factually erroneous, legally
erroneous, procedurally defective, and compounded by Appellee’s total failure to
contest any portion of the record or Appellant’s arguments.
II. CONTINUED COMPOUNDING HARM REQUIRES EXPEDITED RELIEF
5. As documented in Appellant’s personal records and external documentation,
Appellant continues to suffer employment consequences, including removal from
job platforms such as DoorDash, based on the challenged conviction
6. The denial of appellate counsel, the improper dismissal despite indigency, and the
refusal to recognize a lawful appeal—all as outlined in Appellant’s brief—amount
to a continuing deprivation of liberty and access to justice.
7. This Court has the authority under Miss. R. App. P. 2(c) to suspend procedural rules
“to expedite decision, or for other good cause shown,” particularly where failure to
act would result in manifest injustice.
III. PROCEDURAL OBSTRUCTION, UNLAWFUL INCARCERATION, AND
STATE MISCONDUCT WARRANT HEIGHTENED SCRUTINY AND RELIEF
8. On May 2, 2025, the Clerk issued a deficiency notice regarding Appellant’s Record
Excerpts. While technical issues were cited, the Court must consider:
(a) Appellant was incarcerated at Mississippi State Penitentiary (MSP) for the
majority of the briefing period. Under Mississippi appellate practice, incarcerated
appellants proceeding pro se are exempt from the Record Excerpts requirement
unless specifically directed otherwise. While Appellant did not document this
exemption at the time, it is legally applicable and should now excuse any formatting
or procedural defect.
(b) The Record Excerpts cover page explicitly referenced obstruction by the
Lafayette County Circuit Court Clerk, who restricted Appellant’s access to
necessary documents.
9. Appellant’s unlawful sentence was vacated on January 21, 2025, and he was
released on January 22, 2025. During critical phases of the appeal, Appellant had
limited access to legal resources and no appointed counsel.
10. Meanwhile, the Attorney General’s Office was properly served and had full access
to Appellant’s brief. In prior post-conviction appeals, it was the Attorney General—
not the local prosecutor—who submitted the Appellee’s brief. Their failure to
respond reflects deliberate neglect.
11. Mississippi courts have long held that when procedural obstacles stem from
incarceration, obstruction, or lack of counsel, courts must liberally construe
appellate procedure in favor of review. See Kennard v. State, 127 So.2d 848 (Miss.
1961); Caston v. State, 823 So.2d 473 (Miss. 2002).
12. To allow further delay or inaction would reward bad faith and permit manifest
injustice to persist.
