MOTION TO DISMISS CRIMINAL AFFIDAVIT AND RESPONSE TO MOTION TO REVOKE AND DENY BOND 
AND HOLD DEFENDANT UNTIL HEARING ON THE SAME 
COMES NOW Matthew Reardon, by and through court-appointed counsel, and responds 
to the above-referenced motion, as follows: 
1. 
This Court is familiar with Matthew Reardon and knows that in the nearly ten years 
of dealing with him that he has never engaged in any violent conduct whatsoever. He has never 
committed an act of violence, never been charged with a violent crime, nor ever been accused of 
committing an act of violence. 
2. 
The present matter involves Mr. Reardon's arrest for being on the premises of 
Oxford City Hall. Mr. Reardon is well-known to be a citizen journalist and numerous people, 
including local law enforcement, follow his citizen journalism on and through social media. "First 
Amendment Audits" are a growing trend among citizen journalist wherein they exercise their 
constitutional right to access public spaces and take video recordings in those spaces as allowed 
by the Constitution. (See Wikipedia on First Amendment Audits attached hereto as Exhibit "A"). 
Mr. Reardon has previously engaged in several First Amendment Audits and has posted those 
Audits on social media. 
3. 
Mr. Reardon was conducting a First Amendment Audit of the Oxford City Hall's 
publicly accessible premises when he is alleged to have been stalking the Oxford Mayor. The 
(D1952267.1) 
video will show that he was taking no action toward any person. Mr. Reardon did not ask to see 
any particular person nor did he try to access any private space such as an office. His only physical 
action was his presence alone, action protected by the Constitution. 
4. It took law enforcement over a week to decide if Mr. Reardon should even be 
charged with a crime. If Mr. Reardon's actions evidenced the sort of imminent, serious danger 
that disqualifies and arrestee from bond, it would not have taken a week to decide probable cause. 
Individuals accused of murder, rape, robbery with a deadly weapon and other violent crimes that 
supported by eyewitness testimony and followed by immediate arrest are routinely offered bond. 
This case is a far cry from even those bondable offenses, much less the offenses where bond is 
denied by this Court. Even if Mr. Reardon's presence at City Hall constitutes probable cause for 
the charge of Aggravated Stalking, there is nothing about this situation that warrants holding him 
without bond. 
5. Counsel for Mr. Reardon is unaware of any other case where denial of bond has 
been sought by the District Attorney's office in situations where there has been no violence, no 
threat of violence, no injury, no weapon and where probable cause for felony arrest was so 
debatable that it took eight days to pull the trigger on arrest. 
6. 
The constitutional presumption of innocence commands the right to bail on the least 
onerous conditions, and the federal constitutional right to Pre-Trial Release is guaranteed also in 
every state constitution. Under M.R.C.P. Rule 8.2(a), Mr. Reardon can be denied release only if 
his "being at large will pose a real and present danger to others or to the public at large." Mr. 
Reardon has never posed and does not pose a "danger" to anyone. Herein, no threats were made. 
There was no personal contact. There was no action during the First Amended Audit directed 
toward the Mayor. 
(D1952267.1) 
7. 
Further, M.R.C.P. Rule 8.2(a) requires release even when a "danger" exists. The 
Court is required to release the defendant and impose "the least onerous conditions contained in 
Rule 8.4 that will reasonably assure the defendant's appearance or that will eliminate or minimize 
the risk of harm to others or the public at large." If there is any risk of danger, that risk can be 
eliminated by Rule 8.4 conditions. 
8. 
Holding Mr. Reardon without bond will violate his constitutional rights, including, 
but not limited to, his liberty rights, presumption of innocence, the prohibition of cruel and unusual 
punishment, the prohibition of punishment by or with pretrial detention, substantiative and 
procedural due process rights, and other constitutional rights implicated by confining him for 
conduct that was so benign it took a week for law enforcement and the District Attorney to decide 
to charge him with a felony offense at all. Where the initial bond was set at $10,000.00 and no 
contact, the court is likely just as appalled by the State's request to deny bond as Mr. Reardon is 
and should dispose it and the request by denial and dismissal at the felony arrest at Preliminary 
Hearing. 
9. Mr. Reardon respectfully requests that the District Attorney's Motion be denied 
and that the arrest be dismissed. In the alternative, he requests that he be released on his own. 
recognizance subject to any conditions or limitations the Court may order to eliminate any concern 
the Court has about his conduct during release. 
WHEREFORE, PREMISES CONSIDERED, Matthew Reardon, by and through court- 
appointed counsel, requests the relief identified above and for any further relief that the Court 
deems appropriate.
