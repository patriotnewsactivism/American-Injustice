INTRODUCTION
1. This case arises from (a) a blood-draw warrant procured through judicial deception in
violation of Franks v. Delaware, 438 U.S. 154 (1978); (b) an unreasonable seizure of
Plaintiff s vehicle via impound/tow from private property despite ready alternatives; and
(c) a malicious prosecution culminating in a felony DWI indictment tainted by false
statements and omissions—including out-of-state coordination—later dismissed.
JURISDICTION AND VENUE
2. This Court has federal-question jurisdiction under 28 U.S.C. § 1331. Venue is proper
under 28 U.S.C. § 1391(b)(2) because a substantial part of the events occurred in Galveston
County, Texas.

FACTS
A. The stop; Plaintiffs repeated request for a breath test; zero alcohol confirmed
11. On August 11, 2023, at approximately 1:52-2:11 a.m., Plaintiff was stopped by GPD and
promptly pulled onto private property on/near the 4300 block of Broadway Avenue J in
Galveston. See GPD dashcam (file LarryMurph_2O23o8noi47_2H2_33287i558 at 01:47”) and BWC
logs (Doc. “Reardon Investigation Files,” Body Worn Cameras).
12. From the outset, Plaintiff repeatedly requested to take a breathalyzer test to
demonstrate sobriety. See Osteen BWC (01:50 video; audio on -04:07) and Hall BWC (02:30 and
02:56 sequences). Plaintiff stated he had not consumed alcohol and had been sober for years.
13. GPD officers refused to administer a breath test at the scene or at the station, asserting
they “don’t have” portable breathalyzers; instead, they arrested Plaintiff and proceeded to
seek a blood draw. See Osteen BWC 01:50 (breath-test request; arrest); Hall BWC 02:04-02:56
(statutory warning; officers insist on blood draw).
14- The Texas DPS Houston Crime Lab’s alcohol/volatiles report (Laboratory Case HOU-2308-
10286; issued Sept. 6,2023) conclusively states: “No alcohol detected.” Ex. A (DPS Houston
Toxicology, HOU-2308-10286, Alcohol/Volatiles Laboratory Report, issued 9/6/2023; “No alcohol
detected.”Page 1; email distribution includes “wosteen@galvestontx.gov”).
B. Judicial deception in the warrant affidavit (Franks)
15. Before obtaining a warrant, Officer Osteen prepared and swore to an “Affidavit for
Search Warrant” (Ex. B; from Franks motion filings) that included, among other things, the
following material assertions:
a. “Odor of Alcoholic Beverage on breath: Moderate.”
b. “Speech: Slurred & Thick-Tongued.”
c. “Eyes: Watery & Dilated.”
d. “Walking: Heavy Footed.”
e. “Affiant smelled an odor of an unknown alcoholic beverage emitting from the vehicle”
and claimed “watery, glossy eyes.”
16. The affidavit omitted that (i) Plaintiff persistently requested a breath test (a less
intrusive, alcohol-specific test) and (ii) once Plaintiff exited the car, Osteen could not
detect any alcohol odor emitting from Plaintiff himself. In the July 18, 2024 transcript,
Osteen testified: “once Mr. Reardon stepped out of the vehicle... I couldn’t... detect an odor
of an unknown alcoholic beverage emitting from him.” Revocation Transcript atp. 24, lines 1-5
(Ex. C).
17- The affidavit’s assertions were false or made with reckless disregard for the truth. The
DPS Houston alcohol report—available to GPD on Sept. 6, 2023—conclusively found no
alcohol in Plaintiff s blood. Ex. A. It is physically inconsistent for a person with “no alcohol
detected” to have “moderate” alcohol odor on breath and to manifest alcohol-related
impairment signs as sworn.
18. Further, Osteen’s sworn testimony conflicted with his affidavit in key respects: he later
testified that Plaintiff s pupils were “very small” “pinpoint” (Revocation Tr. p. 24, line 10; p. 35,
line 7), contradicting “dilated” in the affidavit; and that Plaintiff was “talking extremely fast”
(Revocation Tr. p. 35, line 8), contradicting “slurred & thick-tongued” in the affidavit.
19. These falsehoods and omissions were essential to the magistrate’s probable-cause
determination. Without them, there was no fair probability that a search for alcohol in
blood would yield evidence of alcohol intoxication—especially given Plaintiff s repeated
offer of a breath test. Franks, 438 U.S. at 171-72; Melton v. Phillips, 875 F.3d 256, 263-66
(5th Cir. 2017) (en banc).
C. Forced execution of the warrant at the hospital
20. At UTMB Hospital, multiple officers forcefully restrained Plaintiff (prone on a hospital
bed) while Defendant McDougle inserted the needle multiple times to obtain blood, despite
Plaintiff s explicit non-consent, fear of needles, and nonstop request to take a breath test
instead. See Osteen BWC 02:56 (escort to ER; prone restraint; multiple sticks); Hall BWC 02:56 (assists
in holding Plaintifff or the blood draw).
21. Officers refused to show Plaintiff the warrant and declined the less intrusive, alcohol­
specific breath test. The manner of execution was unreasonable even if the warrant were
valid. See United States v. Ramirez, 523 U.S. 65 (1998) (manner of search must be
reasonable).
22. Because the warrant was obtained by judicial deception, the blood draw violated the
Fourth Amendment. Franks, 438 U.S. at 171-72.
D. Warrantless impound/tow of the vehicle from private property
23. After Plaintiff s arrest, GPD impounded his vehicle from private property
notwithstanding the presence of friends on site who could immediately take custody. The
car posed no hazard. See dashcam tow sequence “LarryMurph_2O23o8no2i6_2ii2_33287i56i”;
tow arrival at -4:44 on timestamp) and wrecker sheet (Ex. D).
24. On information and belief, the tow decision lacked standardized, non-pretextual
criteria and officers failed to consider readily available alternatives (e.g., release to a friend
on scene). The subsequent disappearance of the keys and Plaintiff s inability to retrieve the
car while jailed compounded the unreasonable seizure.
E. Tainted indictment; later dismissal
25. On Sept. 6, 2023—approximately three months before the Dec. 7, 2023 felony
indictment—GPD received the DPS Houston alcohol report: “No alcohol detected.” Ex. A.
Upon information and belief, there was no confirmed drug analysis in hand by the
indictment date, and the felony hinged upon alleged prior convictions.
26. In parallel, out-of-state coordination occurred. GPD call logs reflect at 2:41 a.m. on
Aug. 11, 2023 a phone call from Lafayette County Sheriff s Deputy Hoby James to GPD to
speak with “the officer that arrested him,” followed by additional Mississippi calls. See
“Reardon Investigation Files,” Call Recordings: 2:41 a.m. (Hoby James), 4:04 a.m. (Deputy
Beavers) and subsequent internal routing.
27. On Dec. 7, 2023, Plaintiff was indicted for felony DWI (third or more). See Ex. E
(Indictment reference). Ultimately, the State dismissed the felony on May 24, 2024 (see
Revocation Tr. p. 16; Defense Ex. D-4 order dismissing felony), and subsequently
dismissed the case “because it could not prove the charges beyond a reasonable doubt” (as
described in Plaintiff’s Feb. 25, 2025 Franks motion).
28. Osteen testified at the revocation hearing that there “was alcohol detected”—contrary
to the DPS lab report, which said “No alcohol detected.’’Revocation Tr.p. 27, line 17(“There was
alcohol detected’’). This false testimony further demonstrates the taint that infected legal
process.
29. The magistrate’s warrant and the grand jury indictment were tainted by Osteen’s false
statements and material omissions; the independent-intermediary doctrine therefore does
not bar relief. See Winfrey v. Rogers, 901 F.3d 483, 494-501 (5th Cir. 2018) (taint exception).
