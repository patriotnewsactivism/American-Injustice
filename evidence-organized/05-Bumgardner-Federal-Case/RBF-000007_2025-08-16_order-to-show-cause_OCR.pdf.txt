ORDER TO SHOW CAUSE
This matter comes before the court on the plaintiffs complaint filed under 42 U.S.C. § 1983.
The plaintiff alleges that the defendants fabricated two charges against her: intimidation of a witness
and retaliation against a public servant. As discussed in detail below, with more supporting facts,
these allegations are the type that may support claims of malicious prosecution. However, it appears
that her allegations fail to state a valid claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. For this reason, the court will
require the plaintiff to show cause, within 21 days, why her allegations, as pled, should not be
dismissed with prejudice for failure to state a claim upon which relief could be granted. Further, the
plaintiff must provide additional factual allegations so the court may conduct an initial review of the
merits of this case.
Factual Allegations1
The facts and circumstances of this case arise out of the arrest and detention of plaintiff
Rebecca Lynn Bumgardner in Lowndes County, Mississippi, on charges of witness intimidation and
retaliation against a public servant. See, generally, Docs. 8, 9, and 10 (plaintiffs complaint and
supplements). She was ultimately convicted for three counts of possession of methamphetamine and
is serving her sentence in the custody of the Mississippi Department of Corrections.

The Factual Allegations Suggest a Claim of Malicious Prosecution
Bumgardner claims that she has been falsely charged with two crimes: intimidation of a
witness and retaliation against a public servant. Construing her allegations liberally, her claim under
42 U.S.C. § 1983, then, is malicious prosecution, which has three elements: (1) the initiation of
criminal proceedings; (2) without probable cause; and (3) the proceedings terminated in the acquittal
or discharge of the accused.6 Thompson v. Clark. 596 U.S. 36,44, 142 S. Ct. 1332, 1338,212 L. Ed.
2d 382 (2022).
As to the first charge, intimidation of a witness. Bumgardner has alleged facts supporting the
first element, as the criminal proceeding was initiated. She has not, however, alleged facts supporting
the other two elements. First, at her initial appearance, the Judge found probable cause to support the
charge, though it is not clear whether the court later held a preliminary hearing, whether the case was

presented to a Grand Jury, or whether a Grand Jury ultimately returned an indictment.7 Bumgardner’s
initial appearance established that probable cause existed to show that she intimidated the witness -
which seems to preclude her claim of malicious prosecution. In addition, it is not clear in her
complaint and supplements whether the criminal proceeding has concluded, as she is currently
incarcerated on other charges. If the proceedings have not terminated, then they cannot have
terminated in her favor. Thus, it appears that she has not alleged facts to support the third element,

Her claims arising out of the second charge, retaliation against a public servant, are similarly
unsupported by her allegations. Again, her factual allegations support the first element - that criminal
proceedings were initiated against her. However, she has not stated whether a warrant was issued or
whether she waived her initial appearance or preliminary hearing as to that charge. In any event, a
Grand Jury later returned an indictment as to the retaliation charge, and the indictment established that
probable cause existed to support the charge. Bumgardner has not alleged facts to show whether - or
how - those proceedings terminated. Hence, she has not alleged that the retaliation proceedings
terminated in her favor. The absence of such factual allegations is fatal to Bumgardner’s claims.

that she must seek redress for her claims in her criminal cases, as “[criminal proceedings and criminal
judgments require criminal remedies—not civil ones.” Wilson v. Midland Cnty., Texas. 116 F.4th

To assist the court with its initial merits review under 28 U.S.C. § 1915A, Bumgardner must

Conclusion
It appears that the plaintiffs allegations do not state a valid claim under 42 U.S.C. § 1983. As
such, the plaintiff is ORDERED to SHOW CAUSE why the instant case should not be dismissed
with prejudice for failure to state a claim upon which relief could be granted. The plaintiff is also
ORDERED to provide the court with the additional information, described above, to aid in its initial
merits review. The deadline for the plaintiff to respond is 21 days from the date of this order.
