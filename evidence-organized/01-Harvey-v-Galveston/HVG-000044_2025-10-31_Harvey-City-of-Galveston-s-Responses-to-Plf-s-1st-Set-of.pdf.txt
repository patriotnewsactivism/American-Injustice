THE CITY OF GALVESTON’S RESPONSES
1. Identify all City officials or employees who reviewed or had authority to discipline
Battalion Chief Roy following the Incident, and state the actions taken or intentionally not
taken.
RESPONSE: Chapter 143 of the Texas Local Government Code dictates the required procedure
for imposing discipline against City of Galveston fire department personnel, including Battalion
Chief Roy. The only City of Galveston official who had authority to initiate discipline against
Battalion Chief Roy following the Incident was City of Galveston Fire Chief Mike Varela, Jr. No
City official other than City of Galveston Fire Chief Mike Varela, Jr. had authority to initiate
discipline against Battalion Chief Roy following the Incident.
Under Texas civil service statutes codified in Chapter 143 of the Texas Local Government Code,
adopted by the City of Galveston through The Charter and Code of the City of Galveston, the City
of Galveston Civil Service Commission has the sole final authority to discipline any City of
Galveston fire department employee, including Battalion Chief Roy, and that final authority is
limited application of the controlling provisions of Texas state law as set out in Chapter 143 of the
Texas Local Government Code.
In accordance with his authority under Texas law, City of Galveston Fire Chief Mike Varela, Jr.
initiated a two day disciplinary suspension against Battalion Chief Roy based upon the occurrences
which form the basis of the lawsuit accusing Battalion Chief Roy of violating a Galveston Fire
Department administrative regulation requiring Galveston firefighters to be more considerate
when sounding a siren when a person is near a fire department vehicle.
The two day suspension that Galveston Fire Chief Mike Varela, Jr. initiated became final when
Battalion Chief Roy decided not to challenge the suspension before the City of Galveston Civil
Service Commission.
City of Galveston police officers D.S. Watson, S. Coldiron, and Galveston police department
criminal investigator, Anthony Rogers, performed a criminal investigation of Battalion Chief
Roy’s actions during the occurrences which form the basis of this lawsuit and, in in consultation
with representatives of the Galveston County Criminal District Attorneys’ Office, the police
officers and investigator concluded the evidence did not warrant any charge against Battalion Chief
Roy.

4. Describe all training provided to Battalion Chiefs on interactions with pedestrians while
operating City vehicles.
RESPONSE: Battalion chiefs at the Galveston Fire Department play a critical role in emergency
response and public safety. As senior officers, they not only manage fire scenes and coordinate
personnel but also operate City vehicles in high-stakes environments. Their training reflects the
importance of safe and professional interactions with pedestrians during these operations.
The department ensures that battalion chiefs undergo advanced driver safety training. This includes
defensive driving techniques and emergency vehicle operation under Code 3 conditions, which
involve lights and sirens. Chiefs learn to navigate through pedestrian-heavy zones with heightened
awareness, adhering to Texas state laws and Galveston city ordinances. They practice maneuvering
vehicles in congested areas while minimizing risks to civilians.
In addition to driving skills, battalion chiefs receive instruction in incident command and scene
safety. They take responsibility for securing emergency scenes, which often requires directing
pedestrian movement and positioning vehicles to avoid obstructing foot traffic. Chiefs use spotters,
radios, and visual signals to maintain situational awareness and prevent accidents.
Professional conduct forms another pillar of their training. Chiefs must interact respectfully and
calmly with the public, even under pressure. They learn to communicate clearly with pedestrians,
offering guidance and reassurance during emergencies. The department emphasizes the
importance of maintaining a composed demeanor and avoiding confrontational behavior.
Training sources include certifications from the Texas Commission on Fire Protection (TCFP),
federal emergency vehicle operation courses, and ongoing departmental education. These
programs equip battalion chiefs with the skills and judgment necessary to operate vehicles safely
and engage with pedestrians responsibly.

to an “emergency” within the meaning of Tex. Civ. Prac. & Rem. Code §101.055 at the

RESPONSE: On June 22, 2024, at approximately 12:11 a.m., emergency dispatch received a 911
call reporting a possible structure fire at 1817 61st Street in Galveston, Texas. The initial dispatch
code was for a structure fire, which was later identified as a fire behind a building. The nature of
the call warranted a full emergency response due to the potential risk to structures and public
safety.
Multiple fire suppression units, including Engines E1, E2, E4, E5, GE1–GE5, Ladder L4, and
Tower T1, were dispatched emergently from various stations. Battalion Chief William Roy (Unit

BC-4) also responded to the scene and assumed incident command responsibilities. The fire units
arrived with lights and sirens activated, consistent with standard protocol for fire emergencies.
Fire crews arrived on scene and began searching for the reported fire. Fire crews located a small

a working fire. Firefighters ultimately extinguished the fire with water.
While coordinating the response, Chief Roy maneuvered his vehicle along 61st Street and into the

6. Identify all City employees who had possession, custody, or control of bodycam, dashcam,
or surveillance videos related to Incident No. 24-003697, and explain the disposition of
each recording.
RESPONSE: Lieutenant Jeffrey Banks, a watch commander who received multiple video files
through email. Further, the City of Galveston Police Department Crimes Investigation Division
investigated Plaintiff’s report of an alleged assault by Chief Roy. Members of the CID were
responsible for collecting and preserving video evidence.
The Police Department stores video evidence in secure systems, including the property room,
where an appointed evidence technician maintains custody. This technician ensures all media is
properly documented, protected from damage, and tracked through a detailed chain of custody.
Officers submit video recordings at the end of their shifts, following strict protocols for labeling
and storage.
When video evidence no longer serves an investigative or legal purpose, the department initiates
a disposal process. Disposal occurs only after receiving proper authorization from investigators or
judicial authorities. The department follows retention schedules based on state law, case status,
and prosecutorial requirements. Once approved, the evidence technician securely deletes or
destroys the footage, ensuring compliance with privacy standards and legal mandates. This
structured approach allows the City of Galveston to maintain the integrity of its video evidence
while balancing operational efficiency and legal compliance.
Here, the City did not dispose of any video recordings.
