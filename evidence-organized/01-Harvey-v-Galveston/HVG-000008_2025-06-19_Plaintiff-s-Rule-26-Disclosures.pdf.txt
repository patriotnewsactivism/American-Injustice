expenses that the Plaintiff would not have incurred, but for Defendants’ actions at question

trial.

amounts incurred (whether paid or not) for similar expenditures in the past and calculated
based upon the amount of work estimated to be reasonable and necessary in the future.

General Damages:
In addition to the above-described economic damages, Plaintiff is entitled to general
damages for interference with normal living, interference with enjoyment of life, loss of
capacity to labor and earn money, impairment of bodily health and vigor, and actual pain
and suffering, past and future, and mental anguish, past and future. This issue must be
submitted to a jury to determine the value.
Exemplary Damages and Attorneys’ Fees:
In Smith v. Wade, 461 U.S. 30 (1983), the Supreme Court held that injured parties may
recover exemplary damages from individual defendants in claims arising under 42 U.S.C.
§ 1983. Attorney’s fees may also be recovered against all defendants under 42 U.S.C. §
1988. These issues must be submitted to a jury to determine the value.
