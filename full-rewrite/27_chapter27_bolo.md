CHAPTER 27 — THE BOLO: TWO MONTHS BEFORE

On June 23, 2025, at approximately 4:00 PM, Reardon was recording inside the lobby of the John M. Shaw United States Courthouse in Lafayette, Louisiana. He was asked to leave and refused. He was not arrested. The building closed early that day — at 4:48 PM — specifically, per the record this book has reviewed, to force him out without an arrest.

That detail matters more than it might first appear. A federal courthouse closing nearly an hour ahead of its normal schedule, rather than having its own security simply remove or arrest a non-compliant visitor on the spot, is not the behavior of officials who believed they had a clean, immediate basis to act. It reads more like officials who wanted Reardon gone but were not yet prepared, on June 23, 2025, to make an arrest they were confident would hold up.

What happened next removes any doubt about how seriously federal law enforcement was tracking Reardon by this point, more than two months before the arrest that opens the federal case detailed in the next chapter. The following day, June 24, 2025, the Supervisory Deputy U.S. Marshal for the Western District of Louisiana circulated a report titled "1st Amendment Auditor Matthew Reardon" to court staff at lawd.uscourts.gov, to U.S. Attorney's office personnel, to Constellis — the private court security contractor — and to General Services Administration staff. Two days after that, on June 26, a second incident occurred, this one involving an unnamed companion of Reardon's, in which a Supervisory Deputy U.S. Marshal touched Reardon's back to redirect him toward a posted sign, prompting Reardon's own recorded response: "Don't put your hands on me, motherfucker, that's assault." The same recording captures Reardon's companion challenging two DEA agents — present that day on unrelated official business — for their badge numbers, and a marshal referencing Reardon "stalking a public official in Oxford, MS," to which Reardon replied, "it's my ass." Both incidents were consolidated into a single Field Operational Report, USM Form 210, dated July 16, 2025.

On June 27, 2025, a "District Alert Notice" went out from the U.S. Marshals Service's Northern District of Mississippi office to district court security officers and staff. It described Reardon as banished from Oxford, Mississippi until September 30, 2027 — a reference to the five-year banishment order detailed in Chapter 19 of this book, still in force at the time — labeled him a "1st Amendment auditor," instructed recipients to monitor and notify on his movements, and included a physical description alongside three photographs: one showing Reardon in a tactical vest with a rifle, one a selfie, and one a booking mugshot. The notice was marked Law Enforcement Sensitive. A same-day email specified that BOLO activity connected to Reardon was, at that point, confirmed only across Mississippi and Louisiana — not Texas, not Utah, despite Reardon's own later suspicion that the pattern extended further. A June 30, 2025 follow-up communication predicted, with some specificity, that Reardon would "show in Greenville or Aberdeen sooner than later" — a prediction that would not be borne out for nearly a year, until the June 4, 2026 Aberdeen federal courthouse arrest detailed in Chapter 32, but a prediction nonetheless, made almost exactly a year in advance, by federal officials who had clearly built a working profile of Reardon's habits and likely destinations.

This chapter's core fact bears restating plainly, because it is the single most important piece of context for understanding everything that follows in Part V of this book: by the time Reardon was arrested at the same Lafayette courthouse on August 25, 2025 — the event detailed in the next chapter — federal law enforcement across at least two states had been actively tracking, discussing, and predicting his movements for a full two months. Whatever happened on August 25, 2025 did not happen in a vacuum, and it did not happen because federal officials were reacting, in the moment, to an isolated incident from a stranger. It happened after a sustained, multi-agency, multi-state surveillance and information-sharing effort specifically targeting a man these same documents repeatedly labeled, not as a security threat in the conventional sense, but as a "First Amendment auditor" — a description of an activity that is, whatever one thinks of its wisdom as a practice, protected expression under the same Constitution these federal officials had sworn to uphold.

This is the documentary foundation of the selective-enforcement and entrapment argument that runs through the entirety of Part V of this book, and it is worth stating what it does and does not prove before moving forward. It does not prove that Reardon's August 25 conduct, detailed next, was lawful — that is a separate legal question this book takes up honestly in the following chapters, including the parts of Reardon's own conduct that day this book's layered-thesis commitment requires it to report plainly rather than omit. What the BOLO record does prove is that whatever happened on August 25, 2025 happened to a man federal law enforcement had already spent two months watching, discussing, and predicting — a fact any fair accounting of proportionality, selective enforcement, and entrapment in the events that follow has to reckon with directly.
