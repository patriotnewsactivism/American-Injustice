CHAPTER 7 — A FALSE ACCUSATION WEAPONIZED

Phyllis Marie Crowder is the mother of my oldest daughter, Lydia. By late 2018, Crowder and I had already spent years fighting over custody, visitation, and trust. I was not blameless in the hostility between us. The record from those years contains my anger as well as hers.

What happened in November 2018 is different because an outside investigator reconstructed it from records that still survive.

On November 25, 2018, Crowder and I had a sexual encounter at my residence in Olive Branch. Four days later, on November 29, Olive Branch police were dispatched to Methodist Hospital after Crowder reported that I had sexually assaulted her during that encounter.

The police file preserves both accounts.

Crowder told police the sexual contact was not consensual. In a November 30 interview with Detective Ashley Holladay, she described the encounter in detail and continued to characterize it as sexual assault. The report also records circumstances surrounding the encounter, including that she voluntarily removed her clothing, performed oral sex, later left my residence by herself, drove to McDonald's, and returned to my residence afterward.

Those facts do not, by themselves, legally decide consent. A person can leave and return, or perform one sexual act voluntarily, without consenting to every other act. The reason this investigation ended differently is the digital evidence Holladay obtained later.

On December 13, 2018, Holladay interviewed me after reading me my Miranda rights. I told him that Crowder and I had texted and planned for her to come to my residence to have sex and that the encounter was consensual. I gave him printed screenshots of the text conversations.

On December 18, I brought my phone to the Olive Branch Police Department. Holladay obtained a search warrant for it and had the device downloaded.

His written conclusion is the most important sentence in this chapter:

"The downloaded information matched the printed texts Reardon had previously provided and supported Reardon's claims that this was a consensual sexual encounter. Based on the evidence, I found the allegation of rape to be a false report intentionally filed by Crowder."

That is not my characterization of the detective's work. It is Holladay's own conclusion in the police narrative.

It also corrects an overstatement from earlier versions of this book. The text messages did not somehow make me the sole legal decision-maker on whether a rape occurred. Holladay was the investigator. He compared my screenshots with the downloaded contents of my phone, considered the competing accounts, and concluded that the evidence supported my claim of a consensual encounter and that Crowder intentionally filed a false rape report.

The false-reporting case was formally opened on February 13, 2019. Earlier drafts sometimes placed the charge in January. The police record gives the better date: February 13, 2019, report number 2019004743, nature of incident "False Report Of Crime," summary "False Report of Rape."

The general affidavit supporting warrant number 31808 likewise states that Crowder knowingly reported a crime she knew to be false and incorporates Holladay's investigation, including the phone download and his conclusion.

The warrant was not served immediately.

On January 25, 2020 — more than eleven months after the false-reporting case was opened — Olive Branch Patrol Officer S. George met Lafayette County officers on Highway 7 near the Marshall County line and took custody of Crowder on warrant 31808. The arrest report says she was processed at Olive Branch Police Department, bonded out through No Limit Bail Bonds, and given a February 11 municipal-court date. Her bond was $2,500.

Those dates are important because they replace a vague story with an actual chronology:

November 25, 2018: the sexual encounter.

November 29: Crowder reported sexual assault.

November 30: Holladay interviewed Crowder.

December 13: Holladay interviewed me and received the printed text screenshots.

December 18: my phone was surrendered under warrant and downloaded.

February 13, 2019: the false-reporting case and warrant were generated from Holladay's investigation.

January 25, 2020: Crowder was arrested on that warrant.

I have other accusations and theories about what happened around this period — about Crowder's husband, recurring CPS reports, and what I believed were relationships between Crowder and people in Oxford. Some of those claims appear in sworn filings I later made. Some appear on recordings. They are not necessary to establish the false-reporting case, and mixing them into this chapter only weakens the record Holladay created.

The police file is enough.

It is also important to give Olive Branch Police credit where the record supports it. Holladay did not simply choose my story over Crowder's because I insisted I was innocent. He obtained the phone, compared the downloaded data with the screenshots I had supplied, and wrote the conclusion quoted above. In a book largely about records that I believe were distorted or ignored, this is an example of a record being built through actual evidence comparison.

That does not mean every later decision in the case was clean. It means the investigation itself produced a clear documentary finding.

Nearly two years after the original rape report, Crowder would enter a no-contest plea in Olive Branch Municipal Court to false reporting of a crime. The sentence, appeal, and remand became another long procedural fight — and earlier versions of this manuscript got several of those details wrong.

The next chapter corrects them from the municipal and county-court records.
