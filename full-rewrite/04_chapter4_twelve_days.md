CHAPTER 4 — THE SETUP: TWELVE DAYS

The twelve-day problem does not begin with the May 26 arrest warrant. It begins with a later charging document.

That distinction is important enough to put in the first paragraph because I blurred it in earlier versions of this story.

The surviving Lafayette County Sheriff's Office narrative says Investigator Jared Bundren began examining the Lynch complaint on May 24, 2017. He wrote that he had been told there were "a lot of threats on facebook" directed toward Frank & Marlee's and Todd Lynch, that he reviewed my Facebook page, and that he saw "several videos and posts of Matt with guns verbally making threats toward Todd and his business." He wrote that Todd Lynch wanted charges pursued.

That is the initiating police record I currently have.

It does not say that I displayed an AR-15 on May 8. It does not identify the Del-Ton rifle I bought from Larry Watkins. It does not contain the specific date that later became the center of my post-conviction argument.

On the same date, May 24, a hair-follicle test connected to my custody case came back positive for marijuana and alcohol. I am not hiding that result. It was part of the factual environment in which people around me were assessing my conduct. What it does not establish is that any particular Facebook statement satisfied the elements of aggravated stalking.

On May 25, I met with FBI personnel in Oxford. I have spent years trying to reconstruct exactly what the Bureau retained from that meeting. I have referred to an FBI 302 in later filings and broadcasts, but I do not presently have the 302 itself. I therefore treat the meeting as documented through my own contemporaneous and later records while treating the missing 302 as exactly that: a missing source, not a document I can quote.

Later that evening I sent a final legal demand to the Marine Corps concerning my pending FOIA request for Marine Combat Training records. I gave the Corps twenty-four hours. The next morning, May 26, at 9:13 a.m., the FOIA request identified in my records as DON-USMC-2017-005333 received a final disposition marked "Full Grant."

Those Marine Corps records are part of the prologue. I cannot prove that the timing of the FOIA response had anything to do with what happened later on May 26 in Oxford. It belongs in the chronology because it occurred that day, not because I can establish coordination between federal and local agencies.

By Friday evening, I was under arrest for aggravated stalking.

Contemporary reporting from the Oxford Eagle confirms that Lafayette County charged me with aggravated stalking on May 26 and quoted Chief Investigator Alan Wilburn saying I was then charged with one count. The article also reported that, because of the Memorial Day weekend, I would not go before a judge for a bond hearing until Tuesday.

My own account of the arrest is more specific. I was inside my vehicle and opening the door when it was pulled open from outside. Officers put hands on me and took me into custody. No weapons were drawn. I have previously described the arrest with more cinematic details — being lured, thrown face-first onto scorching asphalt, a knee driven into my spine, assigned tactical roles for each official, and dialogue I cannot source to a recording. I am removing those details because I cannot support them to the standard this book requires.

The officials I remember at the arrest were Lafayette County command personnel Scott Mills, Alan Wilburn, and Jared Bundren, with Oxford Police Chief Joey East present. The point is not to assign each man a role I cannot prove. It is that senior local law-enforcement figures were present for the arrest.

I also need to draw a line around what I know about Phyllis Crowder. Crowder was involved in a separate custody fight with me, and filings in that case were occurring during the same period. But I do not know that she had any involvement in Todd Lynch's accusation or in the decision to arrest me on May 26. Earlier narrative theories sometimes placed her inside that mechanism. I cannot prove that, and I am not going to write it as fact.

Todd Lynch's role is different because the sheriff's report identifies him. Bundren wrote that Todd wanted to file charges and did so after the investigator reviewed the online material. My understanding is that Lynch signed the initiating accusation. Whatever one thinks of the merits, that is the documented connection between the Lynch dispute and the criminal case.

The arrest then produced a procedural problem of its own. I was taken into custody on Friday, May 26, and did not receive my initial appearance until Tuesday, May 30 — roughly four days later. In later filings I argued that this violated Mississippi's forty-eight-hour rule for a person taken into custody. The legal consequence of that delay has been litigated and argued in different ways over the years; the underlying dates are the important point here.

At the May 30 appearance, the court set bond at $150,000. I tried to present what I believed was exculpatory evidence. The court advised me about my right to remain silent rather than turning the initial appearance into an evidentiary hearing. I spent the weeks that followed in custody.

The Sheriff's Office narrative that now survives was itself created May 30, four days after the arrest. That does not make it false. It does mean that it is a post-arrest narrative describing the investigative events of May 24 rather than a contemporaneous affidavit I can identify as the paper shown to a judge before I was arrested.

And that is where the missing-document problem begins.

I have repeatedly requested the original affidavit, complaint, or warrant application that supplied probable cause for the May 26 arrest. The record set I have does not contain an initiating document that reproduces the later "on or before May 8" AR-15 allegation. Without that document, I cannot honestly say that the officers arrested me on May 26 because they had sworn that I used an AR-15 on May 8. That was a claim made too strongly in earlier drafts.

What I can prove is what the prosecution formally charged later.

The Criminal Information in Cause No. LK17-295 is captioned "Aggravated Stalking" under Mississippi Code § 97-3-107(2). On the face of the document, a typed date was crossed out. The handwritten replacement reads: "or before May 8, 2017." The charging paragraph says that I committed stalking while displaying a deadly weapon, "to wit: an AR15 rifle," with intent to place Todd and/or Ashley Lynch in reasonable fear of great bodily injury.

The document was filed July 6, 2017.

The bill of sale for the Del-Ton AR-15 I rely on is dated May 20, 2017. It identifies Larry D. Watkins as seller, me as buyer, serial number S086662, and a $550 purchase price.

May 8 to May 20 is twelve days.

That is the contradiction the documents establish.

It still needs to be stated carefully. The bill of sale proves a May 20 transaction for a particular Del-Ton rifle. The Criminal Information says only "an AR15 rifle" and does not list a serial number. The bill of sale therefore does not, by itself, prove that no AR-15 of any kind could have been available to me before May 20. My position has always been that this was the rifle at issue and that I did not possess the alleged weapon on May 8. But the document itself proves the purchase date of the identified rifle, not the nonexistence of every possible AR-pattern rifle before that date.

That narrower description is still significant. If the prosecution's weapon theory concerned the Watkins rifle, the dates cannot both be right. If it concerned some other AR-15, the charging papers do not identify it. Either way, the formal Information leaves a factual question that should have been tested with evidence rather than buried under a plea.

There is also an important distinction between Bundren's May 24 narrative and the July 6 Information. Bundren described multiple Facebook videos and posts involving guns and statements he characterized as threats. The Information distilled the case into a statutory allegation involving an AR-15 and a handwritten date. The later document is not simply a verbatim copy of the earlier report.

That distinction makes the case harder to summarize in a slogan, but it makes the record stronger. I do not have to claim the entire complaint was invented from nothing. I can acknowledge that I made abrasive public statements, that firearms appeared in some videos, that Todd Lynch wanted charges pursued, and that Bundren described some of what he saw as threats. Then I can ask the narrower question the paper itself raises: how did that body of material become a formal AR-15 allegation dated "on or before May 8"?

I still do not have a satisfactory documentary answer.

The other disputes surrounding late May belong where their records place them. The Tannehills had obtained a separate civil restraining order against me. I made threatening-sounding statements about Rhea Tannehill on livestream that I regret and do not excuse.

The Sheriff's Office also contains a May 30 report written by Major John Hill about what happened after the Tannehill hearing that day. Hill wrote that Chief Deputy Scott Mills and he escorted me from the courtroom and seated me in the back hallway while paperwork was completed. According to Hill, I told Rhea Tannehill, "FUCK YOU. YOU ARE A FUCKING LIAR." Hill then wrote that Rhea started walking toward me with a water bottle in his hand and said, "I'LL BASH YOUR HEAD IN." Hill stepped in front of Rhea, told him to leave, and Rhea walked away. When I said something else that Hill could not hear, Rhea briefly came back around the corner; Hill again told him to leave, and he did.

That report is more precise than the version I told for years. It documents Rhea's threat and Hill's intervention. It does **not** say Hill physically restrained Rhea, pinned him against a wall, or prevented an assault already in progress. It also records my own profanity immediately before Rhea approached. Those details stay in because the report is stronger evidence than my later reconstruction.

That hallway confrontation and the Tannehill civil proceeding help explain the volatility of the period, but they are not the Lynch charging instrument and should not be merged into it.

Likewise, Crowder's family-court litigation added pressure and instability to my life, but I do not know that she caused the Lynch arrest. The temptation to make every contemporaneous conflict part of one coordinated machine is exactly the temptation this edition is trying to resist unless a document actually supplies the connection.

The May 26 arrest left me in the Lafayette County Detention Center until July 6, 2017. That date is independently reflected in my later contemporaneous communications and in the plea paperwork that follows.

July 6 is also the date the Criminal Information carrying the handwritten "or before May 8, 2017" language was filed and the date I entered the plea that ended the immediate prosecution.

The next chapter is about the pressure surrounding that plea: the $150,000 bond, the weeks in custody, attorney Brennan Horan, the Tannehill civil order, and what I agreed to in exchange for getting out.
