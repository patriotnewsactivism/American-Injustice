EPILOGUE — THE RECORD

This book began with a 2016 flag petition and followed what happened afterward through arrests, family-court litigation, criminal prosecutions, civil commitments, public-records fights, federal lawsuits, interstate detention, and appellate proceedings in Mississippi, Texas, Utah, and Louisiana.

The ending is not a verdict that makes every event line up on one side.

It is a record.

On August 7, 2026, Judge Thomas P. LeBlanc sentenced me in the Lafayette federal-courthouse case. After clarifying the sentence on the record, the court placed me on two years of unsupervised probation. Time I had already served would be credited if the probation were later revoked and resentencing occurred. The court imposed no fine and waived the special assessment. It also prohibited my presence at the John M. Shaw Federal Courthouse, other federal courthouses in Louisiana, and adjacent federally owned or leased grounds unless I had legitimate business with a courthouse occupant and gave the U.S. Marshals Service two business days' notice.

My attorney objected that the "legitimate business" condition was vague. The court overruled the objection. The judge also told me that the sentence was not intended to suppress my First Amendment activity and advised me of my right to appeal the conviction and sentence to a United States district judge after entry of judgment.

Three days later, on August 10, 2026, Judge Jeffrey V. Brown adopted Magistrate Judge Andrew M. Edison's recommendation in Reardon v. Osteen and entered final judgment against me in the Texas federal civil-rights case.

I lost that case at the district-court level.

The loss does not erase what the adopted recommendation said. Edison wrote that "the record shows that Osteen lied to Judge Tollison" and called that fact disturbing. The same recommendation concluded that my allegations concerning the later vehicle impoundment stated an unreasonable-seizure claim before recommending qualified immunity. It also rejected my effort to characterize Jeff Busby's affidavit as literally false on the theory I had advanced, relying on evidence that Assistant District Attorney Steven Jubera—not Busby—personally transmitted the March 2024 letter to Texas.

That is what this manuscript has become by the end: not a brief that keeps only the helpful rulings, but a record that keeps the adverse ones too.

The January 2025 post-conviction order did not vacate my underlying 2022 aggravated-stalking conviction. It corrected the July 2024 revocation sentence and led to my release on January 22. The February 24, 2026 Mississippi Court of Appeals decision did not acquit me of the Lafayette County Justice Court misdemeanors. It reversed the Circuit Court's dismissal of my appeal and remanded the matter so the appeal could proceed. The appellate clerk's own records show that Lafayette County Circuit Clerk Jeff Busby had earlier received a record-delinquency notice and then a show-cause notice while that appeal was being assembled.

Those distinctions matter because the strongest version of this story is not the version in which every setback becomes a vindication and every inconsistency becomes corruption.

The strongest version is the one the documents can survive.

The documents establish that the Criminal Information in the 2017 aggravated-stalking case alleged AR-15 conduct "on or before May 8, 2017," while the bill of sale in my records shows that I acquired the rifle on May 20. They establish that the precise May 8 allegation appears in the later charging instrument and plea record; they do not, from the material currently located, establish that the same date appeared in the pre-arrest warrant paperwork used on May 26. That difference is now preserved rather than blurred.

The documents establish that Oxford Police referred the June 20, 2022 City Hall incident to the Mississippi Bureau of Investigation on June 23, that Agent Joey Mauney obtained the arrest warrant on June 27, and that deputies arrested me at the Chancery Court the following morning because they knew I had court there. The Chancery Court was the arrest location, not the conduct that generated the warrant.

The documents establish that the Galveston toxicology testing did not show alcohol and that the DWI prosecution was dismissed on May 24, 2024. They also establish that a federal magistrate later wrote that Osteen lied to Judge Tollison. They do not permit me to call that finding a criminal conviction for perjury, and this book no longer does.

The documents establish that the U.S. Marshals Service was circulating information about me in June 2025, including material labeling me a First Amendment auditor, before the August 25 Lafayette arrest. They do not establish that the BOLO itself caused that arrest. The January 2026 federal judgment separately found that on August 25 I blocked courthouse doors and refused an instruction to move my tripod before I was arrested. Both records belong in the same book.

The documents establish that the New Orleans RTCC camera did not capture the physical altercation at City Hall. They do not establish that the footage was deliberately lost. They establish that New Orleans later withheld requested RTCC and 911 material under a pending-criminal-litigation exemption while offering an initial report for a fee. That is different from proving that the City created the criminal case in order to hide the evidence.

The documents also forced corrections to me.

At the Mississippi Governor's Mansion in July 2025, the contemporaneous recording has me telling the supervisor that the trooper did not physically touch me. An earlier manuscript said he struck me, knocked down my phone, and stepped on it. That version is gone.

At sentencing in August 2026, I told the court that I moved my courthouse equipment when Nugent ordered me to do so and was arrested while complying. The written trial findings say I refused, was warned, heard a countdown, and was arrested after failing to move it. This book reports that conflict instead of quietly choosing the version most useful to me.

I also described the government's October 2025 protective-order motion too broadly in public. The filing sought restrictions on dissemination of sensitive discovery, including Marshal–judge communications. It did not itself prove an effort to conceal misconduct. That correction is now part of the manuscript as well.

That is what accountability has to mean if I am going to demand it from anyone else.

I remain the narrator of this book. I am also an interested party in much of the litigation it describes. Those facts make source discipline more important, not less. Memory can explain why an event mattered to me. It cannot be allowed to override a transcript, a signed order, a laboratory report, or a contemporaneous recording when the two disagree.

There are still unresolved matters in this record. Some cases remain subject to further review or proceedings. Some documents have not been located. Some recordings still need direct verification before quotations should be treated as publication-grade. Where the evidence stops, this book stops with it.

I chose the title American Injustice because the question running through these pages is larger than whether every accusation I made was right. The question is what happens when a citizen keeps demanding records, hearings, review, and accountability from institutions that possess far more money, legal expertise, and coercive power than he does—and what those same institutions owe him even when he is angry, profane, difficult, or wrong about part of his own case.

The Constitution does not require a citizen to be likable before the government has to obey it.

It also does not excuse a citizen from telling the truth about his own conduct.

That is the standard I am leaving on the page.
