CHAPTER 28 — THE ALERTS: TWO MONTHS BEFORE

On **June 23, 2025**, I entered the John M. Shaw United States Courthouse in Lafayette, Louisiana and recorded in the lobby.

The U.S. Marshals Service later documented the encounter in two different records: a Court Facility Incident Report dated June 24 and a Field Operational Report dated July 16. Those records are more useful than any reconstruction from memory because they include the government's own account of why I was not arrested and why the building closed.

The June 24 incident report places the encounter at approximately **4:05 p.m.** and identifies the location as the courthouse lobby. It records my position that the lobby was an unrestricted area open to the public during business hours and that I believed no standing order prohibited photography there.

The later Field Operational Report supplies more detail. It says a deputy called a supervisory deputy marshal about an unknown individual recording outside the court-security screening station. I identified myself as "Don," said I had no official courthouse business, and maintained that I had a First Amendment right to record in the public area.

The report also records the government's adverse account of my conduct. It says I insulted deputies and Lafayette police officers, refused requests to leave, and suggested that the supervisory marshal arrest me.

Then comes the sentence that matters most for understanding the first encounter. According to the U.S. Marshals Service's own report, the supervisory deputy told me I **would not be arrested because I had not done anything that warranted an arrest**.

The report says the deputy nevertheless wanted me to leave because I had no official business. I refused.

After discussing the situation with the criminal chief in the U.S. Attorney's Office and another assistant U.S. attorney, the supervisory marshal decided to close the building because the courts had adjourned for the day. At approximately **4:48 p.m.**, court security officers were instructed to close the building to the public, and I left after being told the courthouse was closing.

Earlier versions of this chapter tried to infer why officials chose closure rather than arrest. The government record already gives a better answer. The marshal wrote that I had done nothing warranting arrest and that he closed the building after the courts had adjourned in order to get me to leave. I do not need to speculate beyond that.

The next day, **June 24 at 4:17 p.m.**, a supervisory deputy U.S. marshal in the Western District of Louisiana circulated an email with the subject **"1st Amendment Auditor Matthew Reardon."** The distribution shown in the FOIA production included federal courthouse personnel and addresses associated with the U.S. Marshals Service, the U.S. Attorney's Office, Constellis, and GSA.

The email said that I had been in the lobby the previous day recording or livestreaming near the security station. It attached a court-security report and a draft report of the incident and asked recipients to remind staff to report **"any suspicious behavior no matter how small"** to USMS personnel, court security, or an outside guard.

That is the first documented internal dissemination in this sequence.

On **June 26**, I returned to the courthouse with another person. The July 16 Field Operational Report added this second encounter to the same narrative.

The government's report says I initially stated that I wanted to file a complaint concerning the supervisory marshal. I was told to contact U.S. Marshals Service headquarters in Washington. The report says I then maintained that I had the right to stand in the public building and record.

The supervisory marshal wrote that he placed a hand on my upper back while directing me toward posted rules. The report quotes my response: **"don't put your hands on me mother fucker, that's assault and I will have to defend myself."**

The report then characterizes my companion and me as loud and disruptive, says our conduct diverted court-security officers from screening duties, and says two people who entered during the confrontation were later identified as DEA agents there on official business.

The report also says the marshal asked whether I had been drinking because, in his description, I was behaving erratically and was difficult to reason with. It quotes me replying, **"I haven't had a drink in 5 years mother fucker,"** and saying I had been with my companion all day.

I am preserving that exchange as the government's recorded account of what was said. I am not using the alcohol question to manufacture a broader pattern or infer a motive that the document does not establish.

The same report says the marshal asked about me **"stalking a public official in Oxford, MS"** because he hoped I would take note of the legal consequences and leave. Whatever the marshal understood about the Mississippi case, the significant point for this chronology is that a federal officer in Louisiana was already discussing my Mississippi history during the June 26 confrontation.

The two encounters were consolidated in the U.S. Marshals Service Field Operational Report dated **July 16, 2025**. Earlier drafts treated the time between the June incidents and the July report as evidence of deliberate intelligence-file construction. The dates alone do not establish why the report was completed on July 16, so that inference is removed.

What the FOIA production does establish is an information-sharing chain that expanded after the Lafayette encounters.

On **June 27**, federal personnel circulated emails concerning me across Mississippi and Louisiana. One message said: **"Apparently this guy is hitting the Federal Courthouses in Mississippi and Louisiana. We will try and Get a BOLO Packet together but until then use this as a FYI to the Guys."**

Another forwarding message to Constellis personnel said: **"FYI; be aware. Once I get a BOLO I will send it out."**

Those words matter because they distinguish what existed at that moment from what was still being prepared. The record shows that officials were discussing a BOLO packet; it should not be rewritten to make every June 27 document itself a BOLO.

What did exist on June 27 was a formal **District Alert Notice** from the U.S. Marshals Service's Northern District of Mississippi.

The notice was marked **LIMITED OFFICIAL USE / LAW ENFORCEMENT SENSITIVE**. Its stated purpose was to inform Northern District operational employees and court security officers about me. It instructed recipients that if I appeared in person they should **"monitor this subject closely"** and notify the redacted contact identified in the notice.

Its background section said that I had recently been frequenting local, state, and federal courthouses to conduct what I described as First Amendment audits. It also said that I had been **"more cooperative"** in the past than my current videos showed and instructed personnel to follow training and local judiciary rules when handling such encounters.

The notice included identifying information and photographs. The FOIA copy redacts the identity of the official who prepared or distributed it. I am therefore not assigning authorship to a specific marshal without a source that does so.

A June 27 email from a supervisory deputy marshal in Northern Mississippi also transmitted conviction documents and stated that I was on unsupervised release. The writer discussed the Mississippi case and asked where the DUI arrest mentioned during the Lafayette encounter had occurred. The same writer said he was reviewing local rules in preparation for the possibility that I might appear at Northern District suboffices.

The correspondence continued on **June 30**.

In one message, the Northern District supervisory deputy wrote: **"I put this out Friday to all my CSOs on REARDON. He is banished from Oxford until September 30, 2027, but I expect he will show up in Greenville or Aberdeen sooner than later."**

That statement is particularly notable in hindsight because I was later arrested at the federal courthouse in Aberdeen in June 2026. The 2025 email proves that a Northern District marshal anticipated the possibility of my appearing there. It does not prove that the later arrest was preplanned a year in advance, and the manuscript will not say that it does.

The June records therefore establish something narrower and stronger than the old chapter's language about a multi-state surveillance apparatus.

They establish that after the June 23 Lafayette courthouse encounter, a Western District supervisory marshal circulated reports concerning me. Within days, federal personnel in Mississippi and Louisiana were forwarding my information, discussing a BOLO packet, sharing photographs, distributing a formal Northern District Alert Notice, reviewing my Mississippi conviction and release status, and instructing court-security personnel to monitor me closely and report my appearance.

The documents also preserve facts that cut against a one-sided account. The June 23 report says I insulted officers and refused to leave after being asked. The June 26 report describes my conduct as disruptive, records my profanity, and says court-security officers' attention was diverted from screening other visitors. Those are the government's allegations about my conduct and belong beside my own position that I was recording in a public courthouse lobby.

What the documents do **not** establish is equally important. They do not establish that every agency receiving an email surveilled me. They do not establish that every later encounter was caused by the alert. They do not establish an unlawful conspiracy. And they do not, standing alone, resolve the First Amendment or courthouse-policy questions surrounding recording inside a federal building.

They do establish notice and information sharing before **August 25, 2025**.

By the time I returned to the Lafayette courthouse that day, the Western District had documented two prior encounters with me, and the Northern District of Mississippi had circulated a law-enforcement-sensitive alert instructing personnel to monitor me closely if I appeared.

The August 25 arrest therefore did not begin with federal officials encountering an unknown visitor for the first time.

That is the factual point this chapter can prove.
