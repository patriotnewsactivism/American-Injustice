CHAPTER 24 — OSTEEN LIED UNDER OATH

The title of this chapter rests on a later federal judicial statement, not on my characterization of Officer William Osteen.

On July 14, 2026, United States Magistrate Judge Andrew M. Edison wrote in *Reardon v. Osteen*: *"I take no pleasure in this result. The record shows that Osteen lied to Judge Tollison. That is disturbing."* The district court later adopted Edison's Report and Recommendation and entered judgment against me on August 10, 2026.

Those two facts belong together. The federal court did not enter judgment for me. It did not convict Osteen of perjury. It did not hold a criminal trial about his testimony. It nevertheless left in the federal record an express judicial statement that Osteen lied to Judge Tollison.

That is the line I am going to hold throughout this chapter. Where Benjamin Campagna's motion accused Osteen of perjury, I will identify it as Campagna's allegation. Where a federal judge later used the word *lied*, I will identify that as the judge's finding. I will not turn either into a criminal adjudication that never occurred.

The comparison begins with the blood-warrant affidavit Osteen signed on August 11, 2023.

The affidavit stated that Officer Larry Murph had observed my vehicle fail to maintain a single marked lane and fail to signal a lane change. Osteen then supplied his own observations. The affidavit said he smelled an alcoholic beverage coming from the vehicle, described my eyes as watery and glossy, marked my walking as **heavy footed**, marked my speech as **slurred and thick-tongued**, marked my eyes as **watery and dilated**, and marked the odor of an alcoholic beverage on my breath as **moderate**. The affidavit also included refusal language stating that after arrest I had refused to provide a requested breath or blood sample.

That document produced the warrant signed at approximately 2:49 a.m.

The later evidence did not line up cleanly with it.

The Texas toxicology report stated **"No alcohol detected."** A separate confirmation analysis reported **0.13 milligrams per liter of methamphetamine**. As Chapter 23 explains, I have disputed that isolated trace result and maintained that it indicated a false positive. Campagna and I have asserted that position from the beginning. But the laboratory did not itself call the result false, and I will not make it say that.

The stronger contradiction does not depend on the methamphetamine dispute at all.

On February 25, 2025, attorney Benjamin S. Campagna filed a motion for a *Franks* hearing in Galveston County Court No. 3. The motion placed Osteen's affidavit beside the DWI case report, the toxicology report, body-camera footage, and Osteen's later sworn testimony from the Mississippi revocation proceeding.

Campagna's filing identified the discrepancies one by one.

The affidavit marked a **moderate odor of alcohol on my breath**. Campagna cited Osteen's body camera for the proposition that Osteen repeatedly said he did not smell alcohol on my breath, only from the vehicle. Campagna also cited Osteen's later sworn testimony that once I stepped out of the vehicle he could not detect an odor of alcohol coming from me.

The affidavit described my eyes as **dilated**. At the July 2024 revocation hearing, Osteen testified that my pupils were **very small** and **pinpoint**.

The affidavit described my speech as **slurred and thick-tongued**. At the revocation hearing, Osteen described me as **talking extremely fast**.

The affidavit described me as **heavy footed**. Campagna argued from the body-camera footage that Osteen had little opportunity to observe my walking before the arrest and later testified that I was **extremely jittery**.

The affidavit used **refusal evidence**. Campagna cited the body-camera footage showing me offering to take a breath test and consenting to being taken into custody for that purpose. The motion alleged that Osteen omitted my willingness to take a breathalyzer so that refusal could be used to support the warrant.

Those are Campagna's arguments, contained in a filed defense motion. They are not findings by the Texas criminal court because the requested *Franks* hearing was never held.

Campagna went further. His motion expressly accused Osteen of perjury at the Mississippi revocation hearing because Osteen testified that the toxicology had detected alcohol even though the laboratory report said no alcohol was detected. That accusation was made by my attorney in an adversarial filing. It was not a criminal conviction or a separate judicial finding of perjury.

The filed motion also documented the body-camera exchange that had mattered to me from the beginning. One of Osteen's first questions was, *"You an auditor man?"* Shortly afterward he told another officer, *"Hey, I'm doing this one, this is an auditor."* Campagna alleged on information and belief that Galveston had a contentious relationship with auditors and argued that Osteen had decided to arrest me before making the observations later used to justify the warrant.

Again, that is the defense theory as pleaded. The recordings establish the words. Motive remains an inference unless a record supplies more.

The DWI case report created another set of internal comparisons.

According to Campagna's motion, the narrative report described a **strong** odor of an alcoholic beverage coming **from the vehicle**. The warrant affidavit, by contrast, marked a **moderate** odor on **my breath**.

The report described my speech as slightly slurred; the affidavit added thick-tongued. The report described my eyes as watery and glossy; the affidavit added dilated. Later, under oath in Mississippi, Osteen described my pupils as pinpoint.

The most damaging feature of those discrepancies is not that I can prove what was in Osteen's mind when he wrote them. I cannot. It is that the documents and testimony can be placed side by side without relying on my memory at all.

That distinction became critical in federal court.

In the 2026 civil litigation, the Galveston defendants' reply acknowledged that Osteen **"stated on camera he could not smell alcohol."** My objections to Magistrate Judge Edison's R&R cited that concession alongside the blood-warrant affidavit's moderate-odor statement.

Edison then addressed the contradictions himself. His ultimate recommendation still went against me. He concluded that even if disputed portions of the affidavit were disregarded, other facts supplied independent probable cause for the blood warrant and the DWI-related seizure. But while reaching that result, Edison wrote the sentence that now titles this chapter: *"The record shows that Osteen lied to Judge Tollison. That is disturbing."*

That is a narrower proposition than the chapter once claimed, and it is more defensible because it is exactly what the federal record says.

There is another piece of the Galveston chronology that needs correction.

I have said at different times that I spent nearly eleven months in the Galveston County Jail because of Osteen's affidavit. That formulation is too broad. The original DWI bond reflected in Osteen's report was **$10,000**. I bonded out several days after the August 11 arrest. I was rearrested on August 17, the same date Mississippi filed its second petition to revoke my suspended sentence. The Texas bond was later raised to **$100,000**, but the Mississippi hold independently prevented my release.

The months that followed therefore resulted from overlapping Texas and Mississippi proceedings, not one document acting alone.

I also need to separate two Texas cases that earlier drafts blurred together.

The felony DWI prosecution was dismissed on **May 24, 2024**. By the time Campagna filed the *Franks* motion in February 2025, the motion itself states that I was charged with **misdemeanor Driving While Intoxicated**, Cause No. MD-0417962, in Galveston County Court No. 3. The *Franks* motion challenged the same August 11 blood-warrant affidavit in that misdemeanor case.

That procedural sequence matters. The felony dismissal did not erase the blood evidence or the warrant dispute. The fight continued in the misdemeanor prosecution, which is why Campagna was still litigating the affidavit in 2025.

I will not claim here that prosecutors reduced the case *in order to avoid* a *Franks* hearing unless a filing or judicial opinion says exactly that. The chronology by itself does not establish motive. What the record establishes is that the felony was dismissed, a misdemeanor prosecution remained, Campagna filed a detailed *Franks* motion, and no *Franks* hearing occurred before the Texas criminal matter ultimately ended.

Campagna's filing remains important because it did what a factual record should do: it put each sworn statement beside the evidence that contradicted it and asked a court to test the discrepancy in an evidentiary hearing.

The Texas court never gave us that hearing.

Mississippi, however, did something else with the same laboratory report. In July 2024, while the Texas felony charge had already been dismissed, Lafayette County put Osteen on a screen before Judge Tollison, admitted the toxicology paperwork, and used the 0.13 mg/L methamphetamine result as part of a second effort to revoke my probation.

That proceeding is where the next chapter goes.
