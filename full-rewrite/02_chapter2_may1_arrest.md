CHAPTER 2 — MAY 1, 2017

On May 1, 2017, I stood near the Confederate monument on the Lafayette County Courthouse Square in Oxford holding the Mississippi state flag. I was armed with a holstered sidearm. I was also recording.

The encounter that followed does not depend on my memory. My recording survives, and Captain Timmy Pruitt wrote his own report about the arrest.

Pruitt's report says Chief Deputy Scott Mills directed him to the courthouse because someone was protesting on county property without a permit. Pruitt wrote that Mills had checked with County Administrator Lisa Carwyle and had been told no permit had been issued.

Pruitt wrote that he recognized me from previous encounters and from Lafayette High School. He also wrote that when he approached, I pulled out my phone, told him I was recording, and that he said recording was fine.

The recording captures the rest.

"You know I am audio and video recording, Mr. Pruitt," I said.

"I don't care. Do you have a permit to be here?"

"Um, the First Amendment actually—"

"Do you have a permit to be here?"

"No."

"All right, you've got to leave."

I asked why.

"'Cause you're going to jail if you don't."

I asked whether he was telling me I would go to jail for standing there holding the flag and demonstrating.

He repeated that I had to leave.

I asked whether I could ask him a question.

"I'm going to ask you one more time to leave. If not, I'm gonna take you to jail. Disorderly conduct."

I told him I would leave and take the matter to my attorney. Then, while walking away, I cursed about the city. I turned back and asked for his badge number.

"You're under arrest," Pruitt said.

"I'm not — I'm leaving. I was asking you — I was leaving."

That is the exchange.

Pruitt's written report describes the same sequence from his perspective. He wrote that he told me repeatedly to leave; that at the count of two I began walking away while cursing loudly; that I made it about fifteen yards; and that I then started back toward him asking for his badge number. He wrote that he arrested me for "DISORDERLY CONDUCT/FAILURE TO COMPLY."

The report also says he handcuffed and disarmed me, that Investigator Nathan Noe arrived to assist, and that Deputy Justin Wilson arrived to transport me. Those details matter because they are better evidence than later retellings about who was present.

I have long focused on the timing of my badge-number question because the arrest followed it immediately on the recording. Pruitt's report frames the arrest differently: as the culmination of repeated orders to leave and my return toward him after initially walking away. Both records belong in the book. A reader should not have to choose between my edited summary and his. The video and the report can be compared directly.

I also need to own what the recording shows about me. I was profane. I was openly challenging Pruitt's authority. I was carrying a sidearm. I believed the permit demand was unconstitutional and said so. None of those facts should be edited out to make the First Amendment question cleaner.

My legal position has always been that I was engaged in protected expression on public property and that asking an officer for identifying information is protected speech. Pruitt's stated basis was failure to obey the order to leave. Whether the county could lawfully require a permit for the particular activity I was conducting, and whether that order could lawfully support the arrest, are legal questions. The factual sequence itself is not hard to reconstruct because both sides preserved it.

I was taken to the Lafayette County Detention Center and later released on a $250 bond. Contemporary reporting identified the charge as disorderly conduct. Some later timeline material also describes resisting arrest, but the May 1 Pruitt narrative in the Sheriff's Office file identifies the incident as disorderly conduct/failure to comply. Until I have a charging document establishing a separate resisting count for this May 1 incident, I am not going to add one simply because it appears in a later summary.

The arrest video spread online. I later said that Pruitt took administrative leave after the video went viral. I have not located a personnel record establishing the formal classification of that time away. My own 2020 messages describe it variously as leave and, more sarcastically, as a "fishing vacation." The publication copy should not turn that into an established disciplinary finding.

The same day also matters in my family case.

Court records show that Crowder, through attorney Mona Pittman, filed emergency custody-related papers on May 1, 2017. A summons connected to those proceedings was served on me that day. In a later sworn filing, I stated that the family-court filing and my arrest occurred not only on the same date but during the same hour.

That "same hour" assertion is my sworn account, not something the current docket material independently timestamps closely enough to prove. I cannot prove that Pruitt's arrest and Crowder's filing were coordinated, and I will not say that they were. The documented point is that the two legal events occurred on the same date. My later belief about the timing and significance of that overlap belongs to me.

There was another thing I believed in those early May days. Crowder told me, according to my recollection, that she had knowledge of a threat against my life. I do not have a recording of that conversation or an independent document establishing who made such a threat. I believed the warning, and it affected how I behaved in the weeks that followed. That is the limit of what I can responsibly state from the current record.

The May 1 arrest was not the aggravated-stalking case. That came later in the month, after the Frank & Marlee's dispute, after a series of public posts and videos, and after Investigator Jared Bundren reviewed material that he described as threatening.

That distinction is critical because an earlier version of this chapter jumped ahead and described the later aggravated-stalking arrest as though the May 8 date in the Criminal Information had already been sworn into the May 26 arrest warrant by the officers who arrested me. The available record does not establish that. The Criminal Information containing the "on or before May 8, 2017" allegation was filed later. The May 26 initiating material and the later charging instrument have to be treated as separate records.

The twelve-day rifle problem is real, but it belongs where the documents actually place it. I bought the AR-15 on May 20. The later Criminal Information alleged conduct involving an AR-15 "on or before May 8." That is a chronological contradiction inside the formal prosecution record. It is not proof, without the missing initiating affidavit, that the May 26 arrest warrant itself contained that exact May 8 allegation.

For May 1, the evidence is simpler: a flag, a permit demand, a recorded order to leave, my profanity, my request for a badge number, Pruitt's arrest decision, and his written report explaining why he made it.

Seven days later, other reports began accumulating. By the end of the month, the stakes would be a felony and a $150,000 bond.
