CHAPTER 10 — THREE YEARS RUNNING

By July 2020, Madelyn and I had begun describing what was happening around our family as a pattern.

I want to be careful about what that means, because an earlier version of this chapter turned our description into more proof than the records support.

During a Child Protective Services home visit in July 2020, which we recorded, Madelyn and I told the caseworker that allegations involving Crowder had recurred "three years in a row." We also described what we believed was a recurring timing pattern around pregnancies and miscarriages in Crowder's life. Those statements are valuable because they show what we were saying in real time, before this book existed. They do not prove that every allegation came through the same CPS hotline, that the timing caused the reports, or that Crowder acted from any particular psychological motive.

The underlying events were not all the same kind of report.

One was a child-welfare allegation I later described under oath as having occurred around 2017. One was the November 2018 rape allegation that Olive Branch police investigated and that Detective Holladay ultimately concluded was an intentionally false report. Another involved Lydia and Madelyn in January 2020 and generated both a CPS contact and a Lafayette County Sheriff's Office report.

That January 2020 report survives.

On January 24, Deputy Brian Howell spoke with Crowder at the Sheriff's Office. His report, Complaint No. L20-000381, is classified as "CHILD ABUSE." Howell wrote that Crowder told him Lydia had been with me from January 17 through January 23 and, after getting into Crowder's car, said: "mommy Maddy hit me really hard because I didn't eat all my sandwich."

Crowder also told Howell that she had contacted a CPS worker identified in the report as Mercedes and that Mercedes had advised her to make a report with the Sheriff's Office.

Howell then called me. His report does not make me look calm. It says I "started going off on Phyllis," told him, "thank you for your time and tell her to kick fucking rocks," and hung up.

That is part of the record too.

The next day, January 25, I called Deputy Joe Quarles and told him Crowder had an outstanding Olive Branch warrant for filing a false police report. Quarles's supplemental report says he checked with Olive Branch, dispatch received a hard copy of the warrant, and the information was passed through supervisors. Crowder was then taken into Olive Branch custody on the false-reporting warrant discussed in Chapter 7.

Those two events — a fresh child-abuse report involving my household and the service of Crowder's older false-reporting warrant — occurred within about a day of each other. That timing intensified my belief that the family conflict and the criminal accusations were feeding each other. It does not, by itself, prove that one caused the other.

On February 14, 2020, Crowder made another report to the Sheriff's Office. Deputy Ethan Tidwell wrote that Crowder said I was harassing her on Facebook, that DHS had an active investigation involving me and my wife, and that she had not allowed me to see Lydia because of that investigation. The same report says Crowder was worried I had "begun stalking her again," but that "she does not have any proof." Tidwell advised her to take the court paperwork and proof of any violation to her lawyer and to consider seeking a protection order through Justice Court.

The phrase "she does not have any proof" is Tidwell's report, not my paraphrase.

A separate Sheriff's Office record from this general period also preserves Crowder telling a deputy: "C.P.S. (Child Protective Services) has told me to end all visitation for him." I disputed that assertion at the time and said CPS personnel had told me otherwise. I have referred on recordings to conversations with CPS that I said disproved Crowder's claim. Until the specific CPS recordings or written agency determination are placed directly into the evidence set, this edition will state the conflict accurately rather than declare the agency's side from a recording I have not yet re-verified.

That is an example of why the phrase "three years running" needs care.

The records do show repeated allegations and reports involving Crowder, me, Madelyn, and Lydia over multiple years. They do not currently establish three identical, malicious CPS reports from the same source on the same schedule. In July 2020, Madelyn and I believed we were seeing a recurring pattern and told the caseworker so. That contemporaneous belief belongs in the memoir. The causal theory we attached to it belongs in the memoir only as our theory.

We also used language during that recorded visit speculating about Crowder's mental health. I am not repeating a lay psychiatric diagnosis as though it were a medical fact. Neither Madelyn nor I was qualified to diagnose Crowder, and I do not have a clinical record doing so. The relevant point is not what label we used under stress. It is that we were trying to persuade a state investigator that the new allegation should be evaluated in the context of a longer history rather than as an isolated event.

That distinction matters for Crowder as much as it does for me. A report that is not substantiated does not automatically prove the reporter knowingly lied. Child-protection systems are supposed to accept reports that may turn out to be mistaken. The November 2018 rape allegation is different because Detective Holladay affirmatively concluded, after examining the phone evidence, that the report was intentionally false. The January 2020 child-abuse report does not carry an equivalent finding in the records I have reviewed for this chapter.

By the end of 2020, I had become consumed with preserving evidence from every direction: police reports, CPS contacts, phone calls, court filings, screenshots, and docket entries. Part of that habit was rational. Some records later proved things I had been unable to prove at the time. Part of it also fed my tendency to interpret separate events as one coordinated system before I had evidence of coordination.

That tension runs through the rest of this book.

I was not imagining every discrepancy. Holladay's false-reporting file existed. The missing 2017 initiating affidavit was a real records gap. The January 2020 Sheriff's reports say exactly what they say. But a collection of real discrepancies does not automatically validate every theory I built around them.

As 2020 became 2021, the focus shifted from family reports and records requests to a traffic stop on an ice-covered Mississippi road.

On February 21, 2021, Deputy Kandi Beavers pulled me over. The charge that followed would eventually become one of the convictions I appealed all the way to the Mississippi Court of Appeals.
