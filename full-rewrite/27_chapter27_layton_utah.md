CHAPTER 27 — LAYTON, UTAH

I moved to Layton, Utah on **February 1, 2025**, ten days after the Mississippi court entered the order correcting my second-revocation sentence and nine days after my actual release from custody.

An earlier version of this chapter opened by saying that I arrived in Utah after Mississippi had fully vacated my 2022 conviction, probation, and five-year banishment. The January 21 order does not say that. As Chapter 26 now explains, the court corrected the July 2024 revocation sentence to the statutory 120-day maximum and ordered my release, but it reinstated the underlying probation and its conditions, including banishment from Lafayette County.

I therefore arrived in Layton with the Mississippi conviction still on the books.

That legal posture matters because four days after I moved into my Layton address, an Oxford, Mississippi police captain was already writing to officials in my new city about me.

I had continued using **Don Matthews** publicly as a journalist and in records work. In the federal complaint I later filed, I swore that when I moved into the Layton residence late on February 1, I had not disclosed the address publicly and had not yet memorized it myself. That is my sworn account. The records establish what happened next; they do not establish by themselves how Oxford police obtained the address.

On **February 3**, I recorded at a Zions Bank branch in Layton. Police were called. The responding Layton patrol officers were professional, and I said so on camera. I had no complaint about their conduct.

Two days later, on **February 5 at 8:09 a.m.**, Oxford Police Captain **Hildon Sessums** sent an email to Layton officials concerning me. In it he identified himself as an Oxford Police captain and FBI National Academy graduate, described me as a former Oxford resident, a convicted felon, and a persistent issue for his department, and said Oxford had discovered a YouTube video of the Layton bank encounter.

The email included my Layton street address. Sessums described the source as **"from our research."**

That phrase became central to my later lawsuit because I had lived at the address for only four days. In my sworn declaration, I asserted that the address was not then available through a legal, official, or publicly accessible source and that I had not supplied it to Layton City. From those facts I alleged that Sessums must have obtained it through surveillance, confidential records, another agency, or misuse of government resources.

That was an allegation. The email proves that Sessums possessed and transmitted the address. My declaration establishes what I swore about its availability. Neither document proves the mechanism by which he obtained it.

At **9:46 a.m.**, roughly ninety-seven minutes after Sessums's email, Layton Police Lieutenant **Riley Richins** forwarded the information through internal city and police distribution channels. My federal complaint identified the recipients as John Does 1 through 139.

Richins's message included a statement that in 2017 I had been arrested for stalking the mayor in Lafayette County. That statement was false. The 2017 aggravated-stalking charge concerned Todd and Ashley Lynch, not the mayor of Oxford.

Richins also summarized the 2022 aggravated-stalking matter as arising after I entered Oxford City Hall with a camera in connection with the Tannehill restraining order. That compressed description was closer to the documented sequence, although Chapter 19 supplies the necessary chronology: the June 20 City Hall visit, June 23 referral to MBI, June 27 warrant, and June 28 arrest.

There is another part of Richins's email that belongs in the record. He told his colleagues that the Layton officers who handled the Zions Bank encounter had acted professionally and that I had expressed appreciation for their professionalism and given them a "pass." The same email therefore transmitted a false statement about my 2017 case while also reporting something favorable about my interaction with Layton officers. Both facts matter.

The address remained the question I could not answer.

I suspected federal involvement because Sessums referenced his FBI National Academy credential. I said so publicly and later in complaints. But the credential itself does not prove that anyone at the FBI supplied the address, and I have not located a record establishing that the FBI was its source. The manuscript will not turn that suspicion into a fact.

My next dispute with Layton involved Utah's public-records process, GRAMA.

The chronology in my own federal complaint is wrong on one date. The complaint says I went to Layton City Hall on **February 20** to follow up on the records from the bank incident. A preserved transcript of the actual interaction identifies it as **Thursday, February 13, 2025**. The transcript itself contains a useful internal check: a city employee says, "Happy Valentine's Day tomorrow." February 13 is therefore the date I am using here.

I first went to City Hall, then across to the police department, then back to City Hall. I was trying to obtain the police report, 911 audio, and body-camera recordings from the Zions Bank encounter. City staff told me the recordings were ready but that I would need to show identification to receive them.

I objected. I was using the name Don Matthews and took the position that a member of the public should not have to produce government identification simply to receive a public record. The recording captures me repeatedly grounding that refusal in my view of the Fourth Amendment.

The recording also shows that the city employees did not simply refuse to engage with me. They explained the fee schedule, directed me between departments, and ultimately referred the identification dispute to City Prosecutor **Mark Arrington**.

That distinction matters because the complaint I later drafted described Arrington in substantially more hostile terms than the recordings support.

On **February 21**, Arrington and I spoke by telephone. I pressed him on how he had connected Don Matthews to Matthew Reardon.

His explanation on the recording was straightforward. He said he watched the video I had published, saw material connecting me to Oxford, Mississippi, and searched for Matthew Reardon. His account did not require access to a secret law-enforcement database. It relied on material I had put online myself.

I had accused him of conducting an illegal search. On the recording, I do not have evidence for that accusation.

I was also the hostile party in part of that exchange. I left a voicemail accusing the city of wasting resources on illegal searches and used profanity while doing it. That recording is part of the record too. I am not removing my own conduct simply because it makes the dispute less one-sided.

On **March 5**, Layton Mayor **Joy Petro** called me in response to concerns I had raised. Her recording likewise does not fit a narrative in which every government official I encountered was acting against me.

Petro treated the inconsistent identification requirement as a legitimate concern and told me she had raised it within the city. She also explained limits on her authority over city personnel. When she relayed what she understood Arrington had told her about identifying me, she referred to Facebook rather than the YouTube/search explanation Arrington had given me directly.

I noticed the discrepancy. I still do not know whether it reflected a genuine difference in Arrington's accounts or simply Petro paraphrasing a conversation. Her own wording included uncertainty. The recordings support noting the discrepancy; they do not support declaring what caused it.

On **March 13**, I submitted a broader GRAMA request seeking calls, emails, and communications involving my name or pseudonym. My later complaint alleged that on March 25 the city produced only part of what I had requested, including the Sessums and Richins emails, while other material remained disputed or withheld.

Those records were how the February 5 communications became evidence rather than suspicion.

On **March 27**, I called Oxford Police Chief **Jeff McCutchen**. In my federal complaint I alleged that I confronted him about Sessums's acquisition of the Layton address, that he did not offer an explanation for its source, and that he refused my request for an internal investigation.

I have not located the underlying recording in the evidence set I am using for this red-team pass. Until I do, those propositions remain what they are in the present record: allegations I made in a signed federal pleading. The manuscript should not convert my characterization of that call into an independently verified transcript.

On **March 31, 2025**, I filed *Reardon v. City of Layton, Utah, et al.*, No. **1:25-cv-00041**, in the United States District Court for the District of Utah.

The defendants included the City of Layton, Layton Police Department, City of Oxford, Oxford Police Department, Sessums, McCutchen, Richins, Arrington, and John Does 1 through 139. I pleaded federal civil-rights and conspiracy theories along with state-law claims, including defamation and privacy-related claims.

A complaint is an allegation document. None of those counts was ever adjudicated on the merits.

The same day, I went to the FBI's Salt Lake City Field Office with a declaration concerning the Oxford-Layton events. In my later sworn account, I said agents would not accept the declaration or discuss the complaint without first obtaining identification for a background check; that I refused; and that agents then ordered me from the visitor parking area under threat of trespass enforcement while one recorded my license plate.

I have described recording portions of that encounter myself. For this manuscript pass, however, the source presently in hand is my sworn declaration and later OIG complaint; I am not treating unreviewed video as if I had independently verified every detail against it.

The federal civil case entered screening immediately.

Magistrate Judge **Jared C. Bennett** temporarily granted my request to proceed in forma pauperis, ordered screening under 28 U.S.C. § 1915(e)(2), tolled the time for service until further order, and restricted further filing while screening was pending.

I filed a motion to lift the stay on **April 9** and an emergency renewed motion on **April 15**. The case had not moved to service.

On **April 23**, I sought to withdraw consent to magistrate jurisdiction and moved for recusal. The clerk issued a notice of non-consent, and the matter was reassigned to District Judge **David Barlow**.

That reassignment was not a judicial finding that Bennett was biased. It followed the non-consent procedure. Earlier drafts blurred those two things; this one does not.

The next date is now confirmed from the project's evidence ledger: **April 25, 2025**. That is when I filed my Notice of Voluntary Dismissal Without Prejudice. The archive identifies the source document as `2025-04-25 - Notice Of Voluntary Dismissal...(Reardon V Layton).pdf`.

My notice relied on Rule 41(a)(1)(A)(i). No defendant had been served or filed an answer. The screening stay had never progressed into an adversarial merits proceeding.

That procedural ending controls how every allegation in this chapter must be described.

The Sessums and Richins emails exist. Their text can be examined. The false statement that my 2017 arrest involved stalking the mayor can be compared with the 2017 charging record. My February interaction with Layton and the Arrington and Petro calls can be compared with preserved transcripts and recordings.

But the central accusations in my complaint — illegal surveillance, conspiracy, retaliatory coordination, unconstitutional acquisition of the Layton address, and municipal liability — were **never tested in discovery and never adjudicated by the Utah federal court**. I dismissed the case before any defendant was required to answer them.

That does not make the underlying emails disappear. It defines what can responsibly be said about them.

Four days after I moved to Utah, an Oxford police captain had my new address and sent it to officials in Layton, saying it came "from our research." Ninety-seven minutes later, a Layton lieutenant circulated the information internally and included a false statement about the crime for which I had been arrested in 2017. Those are document-based facts.

How Sessums obtained the address remains unresolved on the record presently before me.

That unresolved question followed me out of Utah and into the next phase of the story.
