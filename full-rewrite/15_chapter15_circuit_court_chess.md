CHAPTER 15 — CIRCUIT COURT CHESS GAME

On December 3, 2021, I carried my Justice Court challenge into Lafayette County Circuit Court.

I was representing myself. The filing was imperfect — later courts would make that point central to the appeal — but my purpose was explicit. In the final paragraph of the motion I told the court to treat it as notice of my intent to appeal the November judgments. A civil cover sheet filed with it likewise identified the matter as an appeal from Justice Court.

The detail that drove me back to the courthouse three days later was the assigned judge.

In a later sworn filing, I stated that clerk Chyna Sinervo told me on December 3 that the case had been assigned by a random algorithm to Judge Kent Smith. I recorded that interaction. By December 6, when I checked the case again, the assigned judge displayed in the system was John Kelly Luther.

That mattered to me because I had already fought with Luther over my 2020 post-conviction case. It did not, by itself, prove that anyone had manipulated the assignment. Multi-judge circuit courts reassign cases for ordinary administrative reasons. But I wanted to know what had happened to this one, and I went to the Circuit Clerk's office to ask.

An earlier draft of this book said the clerk's-office confrontation was not recorded. That was wrong. The encounter exists on video, and the correction changes how this chapter should be written.

The recording shows me questioning courthouse staff about the assignment. My later court filing says Circuit Clerk Jeff Busby became defensive and instructed staff not to answer further questions. That is my characterization in the filing; the recording itself is the source that should control any word-for-word account of what was said at the counter. The important point is that this was not a scene reconstructed years later from memory. There is contemporaneous video of me trying to get an answer about why the name associated with the case had changed.

What happened outside is recorded too.

Sheriff Joey East arrived and opened the exchange by telling me he had received a call that I was at the courthouse "harassing people." The recording does not establish who made that call. I have alleged in later filings that Busby contacted East, but East's statement on the video does not identify the caller, and I will not turn my inference into the recording's fact.

I answered that I had been petitioning the government. East drew a distinction that is important to preserve accurately. Filing paperwork, he said, was fine. What he told me not to do was go back inside and harass people or cause problems. I disputed that I had harassed anyone and insisted I had only been asking a question.

The exchange deteriorated quickly.

East told me to calm down "before something happens" and repeated that I should not go back into the courthouse to cause a problem. I called the instruction tyrannical. I challenged him about other matters. At the end of the encounter, I told him, in profanity, to go fuck himself.

That last part belongs in the record as much as anything East said. Whatever the legitimacy of my question about the judicial assignment, by the end of the confrontation I was no longer speaking like a citizen calmly requesting an administrative explanation. I was angry, confrontational, and profane directly to the county sheriff. Any account that omits that would falsely sanitize my side of the event.

It is equally important not to stretch East's words beyond what the recording says. He did not tell me that I could never enter a courthouse again. He expressly said filing paperwork was permitted. His direction was that I not return to harass people or cause issues. I disagreed with his premise that my questioning constituted harassment, but the distinction is there on the tape and should not be erased.

The unresolved issue was the one I had come to ask about: why a case I had been told was randomly assigned to Smith now displayed Luther.

I also made a recorded call to court administration about the assignment. The existence of that call matters because it shows the judge-assignment complaint was being raised contemporaneously, not invented later after the commitment proceedings.

The commitment file adds a timing detail that earlier drafts had wrong. The Uniform Civil Commitment Affidavit in Cause No. 2021-691(L) is dated and filed **December 6, 2021** — the same date as the clerk's-office and East encounters — not December 7. The record presently before me does not establish whether Alcorn signed the affidavit before or after the specific recorded confrontation with East. I therefore cannot truthfully write that the East encounter caused the affidavit merely because both occurred on the same date.

The next day, December 7, deputies came to take me into custody on the commitment writ. That seizure is separately captured on a contemporaneous recording.

The defensible chronology is therefore narrower than the version I told for years:

**December 3:** I filed the Justice Court challenge and was told, according to my sworn account and recording, that the case was assigned to Kent Smith by a random process.

**December 6:** I returned after seeing John Kelly Luther associated with the case, questioned courthouse staff on video, and was confronted outside by Sheriff East, who said he had received a call that I was harassing people. The commitment file also shows Rachel Alcorn's Uniform Civil Commitment Affidavit was filed that day.

**December 7:** deputies executed the commitment process and took me into custody.

The inference I drew from that sequence — that the commitment process was retaliation for my courthouse questions — is mine. The record does not permit me to collapse chronology and motive into the same fact.

That distinction became much clearer when I later spoke directly with Alcorn. For years I summarized that conversation as though she had identified Joey East personally as the source of the information behind the commitment. The recording does not support that. She said **local law enforcement** contacted Communicare about what she described as concerning behavior. Later in the call, she also referred to information from "law enforcement and community members." When I supplied East's name, she did not confirm it.

She also gave context I had often left out when telling the story: I was already subject to an outpatient commitment order originating in Calhoun County, and Communicare said its treatment teams had previously tried to engage me. That pre-existing order matters to any fair assessment of why Communicare was involved at all.

Those corrections do not make the timing disappear. They make the question narrower and more defensible.

The next chapter is therefore not the story of a sheriff personally signing a psychiatric affidavit because I asked about a judge. The record does not show that. It is the story of a Communicare supervisor filing a commitment affidavit on the same date as a recorded courthouse confrontation, after Communicare had been engaged by local law enforcement, against the backdrop of an already-existing outpatient commitment order — followed by deputies taking me into custody the next day.

That is what the documents and recordings establish. Anything beyond it has to remain identified as my allegation.
