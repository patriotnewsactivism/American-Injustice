CHAPTER 3 — THE AGENDA

On May 16, 2017, I went to a meeting of the Oxford Board of Aldermen wanting to speak about the Mississippi state flag. I had not put my name on that night's agenda.

Mayor Pat Patterson told me that I was not on the agenda and that I could speak at the next meeting if I followed the procedure. His recorded response was straightforward: "You weren't on the agenda, Matt... You're welcome to come speak June 6th."

I was more confrontational. Outside the building I asked him whether he was a "cultural Marxist." I also objected when another citizen was not allowed to speak, calling City Hall "the taxpayers' house." Looking back at the recording, the cleanest description of that particular dispute is not that Patterson barred me permanently from speaking. He enforced the agenda rule and told me how to get on the next agenda.

I followed that instruction. The published agenda for the June 6 Board of Aldermen meeting later listed item 7: "Matt Reardon to address the board regarding the state flag."

That paper trail matters because events would prevent me from appearing at that meeting. But the May 16 interaction itself should not be rewritten into something the record does not show.

Three days later, on May 19, the conflict moved away from City Hall and into Frank & Marlee's, a piano bar on the Oxford Square owned by Todd and Ashley Lynch.

The encounter inside the bar is disputed. My account has consistently been that Ashley Lynch discarded food I had purchased after refusing to engage with me. The Lynches' account, relayed later in messages and a recorded telephone call, was that my own conduct toward Ashley was abusive and that I was told to leave. I do not have neutral video of every second of the interaction inside the bar that resolves those competing descriptions.

What happened after I left is much easier to establish because I broadcast it.

I went outside and used Facebook Live to complain about the business and call for a boycott. I described the discarded food, urged people not to patronize Frank & Marlee's, and later acknowledged on camera that I had called Ashley Lynch a "bitch." That language is mine. I am not sanitizing it now.

Todd Lynch also contacted me. A recorded call from the period captures an argument in which he defended his wife and business and I made clear that I intended to continue speaking publicly. Whatever constitutional protection attaches to peaceful criticism and boycott advocacy, the relevant factual point is that this was no longer a quiet consumer complaint. It had become a public, personal conflict playing out across livestreams, messages, and phone calls.

On May 20, the day after the bar incident, I bought an AR-15-style Del-Ton rifle from Larry D. Watkins in a private sale. The handwritten bill of sale identifies the rifle by serial number S086662, lists extra parts and ammunition, states a price of $550, and is dated May 20, 2017.

That piece of paper later became important because the formal Criminal Information filed in my aggravated-stalking case alleged that, "on or before May 8, 2017," I committed stalking while displaying "an AR15 rifle" with intent to place Todd and/or Ashley Lynch in reasonable fear of great bodily injury.

I have now inspected the charging instrument itself. The typed date on the form was struck through by hand. Above it is handwritten: "or before May 8, 2017." The document was filed July 6, 2017.

That means the formal charging instrument alleged AR-15 conduct at least twelve days before the dated bill of sale says I acquired this particular rifle.

The strength of that contradiction should not be diluted by overstating what it proves. The Watkins bill of sale is a private handwritten record, not a notarized government record. It is evidence of when I acquired the identified Del-Ton rifle. It does not prove that I had never possessed any AR-15 before May 20. The Criminal Information, however, does not identify a serial number. So the most precise claim is that the State's later charging theory alleged use of "an AR15 rifle" by May 8, while the rifle that became central to my own defense and public account is documented as acquired May 20.

The initiating police narrative is different from that later charging document, and this book previously blurred the two.

Investigator Jarrett Bundren's reporting narrative, Complaint No. L17-05138, describes events beginning May 24. Bundren wrote that he was advised there were "a lot of threats on facebook by Matt Reardon, specifically toward Frank and Marlee's and Todd Lynch." He wrote that he reviewed my Facebook page and saw "several videos and posts of Matt with guns verbally making threats toward Todd and his business." He further wrote that Todd Lynch wanted to file charges and, after Bundren looked further into the threats and harassment, Todd filed charges.

That report is damaging evidence from the State's side, and it belongs in the book exactly because it complicates my argument. The initiating complaint was not described by Bundren as a single peaceful boycott video. He said he saw multiple posts and videos involving guns and what he characterized as verbal threats.

I dispute the criminal characterization of much of my speech, and the recordings themselves are the best evidence for deciding what I actually said. But I cannot honestly write that law enforcement had before it nothing except a customer standing on a sidewalk asking people to boycott a bar. Bundren's own report says he was looking at a wider body of material.

At the same time, Bundren's May 24 narrative does not contain the later specific allegation that I displayed an AR-15 on or before May 8. It does not identify the Watkins rifle. It does not explain how the prosecution later arrived at the handwritten May 8 date in the Criminal Information.

Those are two separate evidentiary questions:

First, what did Bundren and Todd Lynch rely on when the complaint was initiated on May 24?

Second, why did the formal Criminal Information filed July 6 allege AR-15 conduct "on or before May 8, 2017"?

The first question has a surviving sheriff's narrative. The second is where the twelve-day problem lives.

The online attention surrounding me also intensified during this period. A YouTube channel called Reardon Watch reposted and mocked my broadcasts, including material concerning Frank & Marlee's. The existence of that archive is documented. I do not know who operated it, and I do not have evidence establishing that any named law-enforcement officer or public official controlled the channel or made a decision because of it. It is evidence that the dispute had become public spectacle, not proof of official coordination.

The same discipline applies to the rifle purchase. I have said that I bought the rifle because I believed threats had been made against me. That explanation is my account of my motive. The dated bill of sale proves the transaction date, not why I made the purchase.

By May 24, therefore, several things were simultaneously true. I had publicly fought with the Lynches. I had used ugly language toward Ashley Lynch. I had called for a boycott. I had bought an AR-15-style rifle. I had made public videos in which firearms appeared. Bundren wrote that he regarded some of my statements as threats. Todd Lynch asked to pursue charges.

None of those facts should be erased.

Neither should the charging instrument that came later.

The next chapter is about the difference between those two records — the May 24 sheriff's narrative and the July 6 Criminal Information — and about what can actually be proved from the twelve days between the handwritten date on the charge and the handwritten date on the bill of sale.
